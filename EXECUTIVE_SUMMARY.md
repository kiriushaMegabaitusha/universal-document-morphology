# Universal document morphology — executive summary

**v1.0.1 · 2026-10-06 · 28 systems · 60 sources · 92 claims (88 verified)**

## The question

Are there frameworks that fix what goes into an official document — and is
there a universal structure that is *proven* the most efficient carrier of
official information, or just a tangle of traditions?

## The answer

A convergent **deep grammar** exists, across six continents and six centuries:
every operative document decomposes into the same slots — **identity, origin,
date, body/dispositif, authentication, annex**. It is universal *because it
encodes what law requires* (who may act, by what competence, in what form) — not
because it is the most efficient information channel, which no standard
measures. The surface is where traditions genuinely diverge, and the main axis
of variation is **genre** (letter vs normative act), not nation. The claim is
scoped to the *written* official document: two non-documentary record systems
(the Inka khipu, Haudenosaunee wampum) fall outside it.

## The model

14 canonical slots: identity · identifier · origin · authority-mark · temporal ·
destination · reference · subject · setting/preamble · body · dispositif ·
authentication · annex · trace. Two **declensions** by genre:

- Epistolary (correspondence): fills destination + reference, drops the preamble.
- Normative (act): fills the preamble + dispositif, drops the destination.

The fork is a **formal negation**: the preamble (S9) never co-occurs with
authority-mark, addressee, reference, subject, body or trace.

## The catalogue of "kinds of document law"

1. **Layout** — Anglophone / Germanic / Sinosphere (DIN 5008, GB/T 9704, ДСТУ…).
2. **Formulas** — Francophone (banks of formules d'appel / de politesse).
3. **Naming / reference discipline** — UAE (how to name and cite things).
4. **Functional classification** — Soviet/CIS (organisational / dispositive /
   reference-informational; ОКУД type-codes).

Plus three **regime dimensions** beyond the page: **trace** (Thailand, Ukraine),
**access** (Sweden 1766, Kenya), **persistence/appraisal** (Nigeria). And two
**scalar dimensions**: rank (layout → grammar) and secrecy/urgency.

## Headline findings

- **Authentication (S12) is the most universal slot** — 17 of 25 slot-coded
  systems — and the most variable in *form*: hand signature, registered seal
  (hanko), sovereign monogram (tughra), witnessed attestation, cryptographic.
- **Modernity is the identifier**: S2 climbs from 4/10 (1900–99) to 7/9 (2000+);
  digital signatures appear only after 2015.
- **Genre is culturally named**, not universally categorised — five naming
  logics (rank / job / social act / layout / function) and no shared vocabulary.
- **The Soviet bloc legislated all three layers at once** (requisites, type
  taxonomy, workflow) — the densest regime in the corpus, and the ancestor of
  both ГОСТ Р 7.0.97 and ДСТУ 4163 (the four-addressee rule survives it).
