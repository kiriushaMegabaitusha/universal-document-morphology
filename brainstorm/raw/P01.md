## P01-1 — Pre-modern stress test of the genre fork (does S6 ⊥ S9 survive historical mixed genres?)

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Test the atlas's strongest structural claim — the perfect complementary distribution of destination (S6) and preamble (S9) — against pre-modern and early-modern chancery instruments in which a named addressee and a recital coexist (royal letters patent, chancery mandates, farmans addressed to named governors), to determine whether the epistolary/normative fork is universal or an artefact of modern codification.

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: The negative rule was derived from 25 modern systems, all of whose genres were pre-sorted by a national page-format standard or a modern legal taxonomy — and it is precisely modern standards that clean the two genres apart. Historical chancery practice, as diplomatics describes it, routinely produced addressed instruments with full recitals, i.e. exactly the S6+S9 cell that reads as 0 in `analysis/findings.md` §2. Because the pre-modern bin is thin (n = 6) and rests on a handful of systems, the negation has never been exposed to genres that were not pre-sorted, so a dated pre-modern sample is a direct and cheap test of its scope.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- The 14-slot vocabulary maps onto pre-modern instruments without distortion (the atlas already codes the 1412 Islamicate scroll, so medium-flexibility is assumed, not proven for addressed medieval genres).
- Open, citable transcriptions/translations of pre-modern instruments are obtainable by one researcher without a consortium.
- A single coder's S6/S9 judgments are reliable enough that a *pattern* of co-occurrence (across ≥2 traditions, not scattered single cells) would be visible.

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- Pre-modern addressed instruments with recitals produce a non-zero S6×S9 "both present" cell, weakening the perfect negation to a strong but imperfect tendency.
- The co-occurrence concentrates in source-tradition genres whose name means "command/letter to a person" rather than "edict to no one."
- Modern standards-era systems retain the perfect negation — i.e. the fork's scope is historical (a modern regularity), not universal.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: A pre-modern sample of ≥30 dated instruments across ≥3 chancery traditions with S6×S9 both-present = 0 would leave the fork intact and kill the challenge; single counterexamples with ambiguous coding also fail to disconfirm (threshold: recurrence in ≥2 traditions).

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: Linguistic coverage for one person (Latin/Old French, Ottoman Turkish or Arabic, possibly Ge'ez) and dependence on published translations, which may blur the recital/addressee distinction; ~3–5 weeks of manual coding; CPU irrelevant; no participants, no ethics review.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `morphology.md` §3 (the fork as complementary distribution) · `analysis/findings.md` §2 (S6×S9 both = 0) · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §1 (negative rule) · `diversity.md` §11b (royal chancery typology charte / lettre patente / mandement — pre-modern genres already named in the corpus) · `cases/islamic_chancery.md`, `cases/francophone_tradition.md` · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 (pre-1900 bin, n = 6, "thin coding").

**Repo verification** `[idea]`: Grep over all `*.md`, `*.csv`, `*.json` for medieval/charter/letters patent/pre-modern recoding finds only descriptive mentions in `diversity.md` and `cases/francophone_tradition.md`; no pre-modern slot recoding and no historical S6×S9 test exists anywhere in the repo.

## P01-2 — Within-tradition diachrony panel: hold the tradition constant, vary the date

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Build a small longitudinal panel by sampling the same chancery lineage at four or more dates (e.g. French royal chancery → ancien régime → modern French administrative letter; or the Soviet ЄДСД line → post-Soviet RU/UA/EE) and re-coding the same slots at each date, so that the published "modernity = identifier" trend can be separated from a corpus-composition effect.

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: The diachronic claims (S2 rises 4/10 → 7/9; digital signature only after 2015) rest on era bins of n = 6–10 with hand-assigned regime anchors, so era and tradition are confounded — each bin contains *different* systems, and `README.md` already flags the regime classes and era anchors as hand-assigned and not derivable from the CSVs. A within-lineage panel is the historian's standard control: hold legal culture constant, vary only date. If a stem slot changes within a lineage the trend is real; if it appears only between lineages, the published figure is composition rather than diachrony.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- Each chosen lineage yields ≥4 datable specimens standardised enough to code (the corpus already anchors French, Islamicate, Soviet, Chinese and Ethiopian lines).
- Historical specimens can be coded with the same slot definitions as modern standards (shared with P01-1).
- The panel can be written as new analysis in separate files without mutating the frozen `requisites.csv` (the gate must keep passing).

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- S2 (identifier) presence rises within at least two of three sampled lineages — the "modernity" claim survives the control.
- S12 (authentication) stays present at every date in every lineage while its *form* changes (seal → signature → digital) — slot persists, realisation migrates.
- Surface slots (S4 authority-mark, S8 subject) vary more *within* lineages than stem slots do, reproducing the atlas's stem/surface split diachronically rather than cross-sectionally.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: Within-lineage S2 flat or noisy while the cross-sectional trend persists would make "modernity = identifier" an artefact of bin composition; stem slots swinging wildly within a single lineage would weaken the "six centuries, same stem" claim.

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: Specimen-level dating may require reading fonds rather than standards, and one lineage may be too thinly documented for four points; sample will skew to lineages whose languages I read; ~4 weeks; no participants, no ethics review.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 (diachrony table) and §6 (era dating = regime anchor year, small bins, directional not probative) · `README.md` "Honest gaps" (hand-assigned regime classes and era anchors) · `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md` ("modernity is the identifier") · `diversity.md` §11d (French administrative record continuous across the 1960 rupture), §13 (Soviet line 1920 → 1991 → successors), §1 (Islamicate chancery 1412 → 19th c.).

**Repo verification** `[idea]`: Grep for panel/longitudinal/within-tradition returns zero hits in the repo; `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 is cross-sectional only (era × slot shares), and no case file is sampled at more than one date.

## P01-3 — Deep-history extension: code the earliest administrative record objects and give the khipu/wampum boundary an operational threshold

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Extend the atlas's chronology from c. 1412 back to antiquity by coding a small sample of early administrative record objects (cuneiform accounts, Egyptian hieratic dockets, Chinese bronze/bamboo administrative texts, Roman tabulae) on the 14 slots, and use the result to convert the currently qualitative khipu/wampum boundary into an explicit slot-count threshold for what counts as a "document regime".

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: The universality claim is scoped to "the written official document" and bounded by two non-documentary systems, but that boundary is a judgement, not a measurable rule — `diversity.md` §5 says forcing the slots onto the khipu "is itself the finding". Writing began as administrative record-keeping, so antiquity is the one zone where "record system without documents" and "document" had not yet come apart, making it the natural stress test both for the stem's depth and for a proposed threshold (e.g. a regime counts as documentary iff it fills ≥k of S1/S3/S5/S10/S12). One cannot test a boundary rule without cases near the boundary, and the corpus currently has only two.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- Open translations and museum/edition catalogues suffice to code slots without philological specialisation (no assumption of reading cuneiform; coded from published renderings).
- The slot vocabulary is medium-neutral — already assumed for the scroll and the knotted cord, but clay/bamboo will push it further.
- ~20–40 objects across ≥3 traditions are codeable by one person in weeks, not years.

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- Origin (S3), temporal (S5) and authority/authenticate marks (S4/S12) appear in most early administrative objects; identifier (S2) and destination (S6) appear rarely or in recognisably different form (seal-cylinder impression rather than number line).
- A non-trivial share of early objects fall *below* the stem threshold, looking more like the khipu than like a charter — i.e. the boundary is a continuum, not a line, and modern "document" is one end of it.
- The coding forces an explicit decision about whether impressed seals count as S12; if they do, "authentication is the most universal slot" gains the deepest possible support.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: Early objects routinely filling the full stem (S12 + S10/S11 + S3/S5) would push the deep grammar back millennia and defeat the continuum expectation; inability to code the objects at all would show the slot vocabulary is *not* medium-neutral and would indict the boundary claim rather than the stem.

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: Highest philological risk of the six; restriction to objects with published translations skews the sample toward the best-published traditions (Mesopotamia/Egypt) and away from East/South Asia; risk of anachronistic coding of "authentication" on clay; ~4–6 weeks; no participants, no ethics review.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `diversity.md` §5 (khipu as the model's boundary), §12e (wampum: authority does not require writing), §10e (Ethiopia as the pre-modern non-colonial chancery) · `morphology.md` §0/§5 (six centuries, universal stem) · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 (pre-1900 bin: n = 6, thin) · `README.md` coverage line "c. 1412–2024" and the scoped-universality claim in `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md`.

**Repo verification** `[idea]`: Grep for antiquity/cuneiform/BCE/millennium/earliest returns zero hits across `*.md`, `*.csv`, `*.json`; `cases/` (27 files) contains no pre-1412 system, and the two non-documentary boundaries are the only deliberately coded edge cases.

## P01-4 — Authentication diachrony: test whether S12 forms succeed each other in an ordered sequence

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Convert the atlas's static finding that authentication (S12) is the most variable slot into a dated transition analysis, asking whether authentication forms follow a restricted, non-random order of succession (witnessed attestation → registered seal → hand signature → registered stamp → cryptographic) and whether regulatory change leads or lags the material change.

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: `diversity.md` §3 presents five co-existing realisations and `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §5 calls authentication "the migrating slot", but the repo never dates those realisations beyond a single cross-sectional note (digital signature "only after 2015"). Dating each form's adoption per system turns that metaphor into a testable process claim — and sequencing changing authentication practice across regimes is the core comparative method of diplomatics, so this is the lens-native version of the atlas's most-cited finding. A restricted path set would show a process; equiprobable transitions would show the "migration" is just variation.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- Adoption dates (decade precision) are datable from standards/regulations already in `sources/` or retrievable later this round cycle.
- "Form of S12" per system-date is codable by one person against an explicit codebook (witness / seal / signature / registered stamp / endorsement / cryptographic).
- ~40–80 system×date cells suffice for a descriptive transition-frequency analysis (no formal process model claimed).

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- Observed transitions concentrate in a small set of recurring paths, with reversals rarer than forward moves.
- "Leapfrog" cases (seal → e-signature, skipping the signature era) cluster in late developers, consistent with the atlas's diffusion finding (`diversity.md` §12b).
- S12 *presence* stays at or above its current 17/25 share at every date: form varies, presence persists.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: Transitions effectively random — reversals as common as forward moves, no recurring path — would reduce "the migrating slot" to a metaphor; S12 presence falling systematically in older specimens would make "most universal slot" an era-bounded claim instead.

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: Dating adoption per system may need sources not yet retrieved (literature search is out of bounds this round); cells will be unevenly filled (some systems have one date), reproducing the small-n critique the current diachrony already carries; "form" coding may be finer than the evidence supports; ~3–4 weeks; no participants, no ethics review.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `diversity.md` §3 (five realisations of S12), §11a (rank in grammar) and §13c (automation written into doctrine, 1973) · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4–§5 (digital signature post-2015; authentication as the migrating slot) · `analysis/findings.md` §1 (S12 = 17/25) · `README.md` key finding 1 · `morphology.md` §5.1 (authentication as the variable stem slot).

**Repo verification** `[idea]`: Grep for seal/signature transition and sequencing terms returns zero hits; `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 gives only presence shares per era band, and no CSV codes S12 *form*, only presence.

## P01-5 — The record unit above the document: a comparative dossier/file grammar

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Generalise the atlas's single observation that India's CSMOP makes the *file*, not the document, the accountable unit into a comparative test across bureaucracies that visibly keep dossiers (Russian/post-Soviet дело practice, Ottoman register practice, Chinese 卷, Ethiopian imperial custody), asking whether a second, higher-level grammar of assembly exists and whether it converges as strongly as the document's.

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: The atlas is explicitly document-centric and flags the file as a possible larger unit (`diversity.md` §6, `morphology.md` §8) but never tests it beyond India — whereas the bond between items in a dossier (order, docketing, cover summary, pagination across items) is exactly the terrain of archival diplomatics and provenance research. If most large secretariat traditions converge on a file anatomy, the project's unit of analysis sits one level too low, the way the khipu showed the unit could sit elsewhere; if they do not, India's four-part file is local colour and the document-centred model is vindicated by a real test rather than by default.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- File-level practice is documented in records manuals obtainable by one researcher (the corpus already holds India's CSMOP and Ukraine's делопроизводство, plus `sources/`).
- A file grammar can be decomposed into a small closed vocabulary (6–10 file-level slots) by analogy with the 14 document slots.
- Systems without dossiers (Estonia's automatic logs, the private-sector DIN letter) code as "no file unit" rather than as missing data.

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- ≥4 of 6 secretariat traditions show a recurring file anatomy — cover/summary note + correspondence portion + annex + file number — with India's four-part structure reappearing under different names.
- File-level identifiers are faceted (org/year/serial) in exactly the traditions whose document-level S2 is already database-like.
- File-unit density tracks the trace/audit dimension (S14, `diversity.md` §10b) rather than genre — the higher unit belongs to the regime layer, not the page layer.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: File anatomy idiosyncratic per tradition, with no recurring parts across ≥4 systems, would show the larger unit is a local Indian feature; file structure uncorrelated with S14 density would defeat the mechanism claim even if some convergence appeared.

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: File-level documentation lives in records manuals rather than formatting standards, so it is retrieval-heavy and uneven; several traditions may be describable only through scholarship that cannot be cited until a later round; single-coder risk on a brand-new vocabulary; ~6–10 manuals to obtain and code; no participants, no ethics review.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `diversity.md` §6 (file/note finding), §10b (trace/access/appraisal dimensions), §10d (four custody models), §13a–b (Soviet workflow layer, ОКУД as schema) · `morphology.md` §8 (open thread: "the file as a unit larger than the document") · `analysis/findings.md` §3 (coding density by regime class, where records regimes score low by design) · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §5 (synthesis).

**Repo verification** `[idea]`: Grep for dossier/"file as"/defter finds only the Indian §6 passage and the open-thread bullet; no comparative file-level analysis, no file-level CSV, and no second-system case file treats the dossier as the unit.

## P01-6 — Internal diplomatic criticism: machine re-derivation of the slot coding from the retrieved primary texts

Participant: P01 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

**Statement** `[idea]`: Apply diplomatics' own internal/external criticism to the atlas itself: write a CPU-only extractor that reads the retrieved primary texts in `sources/` (60 sources with extracted `.txt`) and independently re-derives slot presence per system, then adjudicate every disagreement against the originals as a formal audit of the hand-coded `requisites.csv`.

**Mechanism / rationale** `[idea]`: The project's own honest gaps say theme coding is keyword-based with no second coder and no kappa, and that regime classes and era anchors are hand-assigned; meanwhile `check_claims.py` and `analysis/recompute.py` validate schema, citations and internal consistency but never re-derive the data from sources, so a systematic miscoding could pass every gate. A deliberately dumb, rule-based extractor (signature blocks, addressee and enclosure lines, "whereas/recalling/依据" openers, reference lines, seal markers) is the independent second reader a sole researcher otherwise cannot have, and its disagreements localise exactly which slots the model's conclusions rest on — the same move as the fingerprint check that already produced an honest result in `diversity.md` §12b.

**Assumptions** `[assumption]`:
- The extracted `.txt` files in `sources/` are clean enough for rule-based extraction (they include HTML dumps and Cyrillic/Arabic/Han texts — encoding and noise are assumed manageable).
- Disagreement will be interpretable (roughly ≤30% of system×slot cells) rather than near-total.
- I can adjudicate all disagreements from the original PDFs within days, without a second human coder — no participants, no ethics review, no consortium.

**Predicted observations** `[prediction]`:
- Agreement ≥ ~85% of cells on perceptual slots (S12 signature blocks, S6 addressee, S13 enclosures) with disagreement concentrated on interpretive slots (S9 vs S10, S11 vs S10, S4 presence) — the boundaries the implication rules already treat as delicate.
- At least one discrepancy worth correcting surfaces in `requisites.csv` or a case file (existence prediction only, no size claimed).
- Extraction yield tracks grounding tier: primary-anchored systems (UA, CN, IN, BR) yield far more extractable slot evidence than the encyclopedia-thin cases (PE, HN, ET), mirroring `analysis/findings.md` §5.

**What would disconfirm it** `[prediction]`: Agreement below ~70% or disagreement scattered uniformly across slots would mean the hand-coded matrix is not reproducible by a text-level reader, putting cell-dependent findings (S12 = 17/25, S6 ⊥ S9) in need of re-derivation before any extension; agreement near 100% would leave the audit with little to add — itself a usable robustness result rather than a failure.

**Main feasibility risk** `[assumption]`: Multi-script and OCR/HTML noise; the real danger is circularity — rules written from my own reading of the codebook would merely mirror it, so rules must be written blind to `requisites.csv` cells; ~1–2 weeks, CPU-only, zero budget.

**Connects to** `[idea]`: `README.md` "Honest gaps" (keyword theme coding, no inter-rater kappa; hand-assigned regime classes/era anchors) and the "gate" section · `analysis/recompute.py` (rebuilds figures from frozen data only — this idea adds a source-level data gate) · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §6 (method caveats: inherits coding sparsity) · `analysis/findings.md` §5 and §7 (evidence base: 60 sources, 57 retrieved; "some similarity is a sparsity artefact") · `ledger.json` / `check_claims.py` (citation gate precedent) · `diversity.md` §12b (fingerprint-before-trusting-a-source method precedent).
