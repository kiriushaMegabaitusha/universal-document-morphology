## P02-1 — Machine second coder for the slot and theme coding

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Build an automatic tagger that assigns the 14 canonical slots (and, secondarily, the 10 theme codes) to each requisite row and to raw document text, and run it as an independent second coder against the hand-coded `requisites.csv`, reporting agreement instead of trust.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** Every quantitative figure in the atlas is derived from one hand-mapped 225-row CSV and keyword-based theme coding that the repo itself flags as having no inter-rater kappa and no second coder — so the analytical load rests on a single unmeasured judgement. A tagger whose prompt/feature set is built only from the slot definitions in `morphology.md` §2 (never from the hand label) yields per-slot Cohen's κ overall and per script family, converting "trusted hand code" into a measured quantity; span-level tagging of `sources/*.txt` then extends the census from standards rows to whole documents. It also substitutes for the unavailable human second coder the repo lists under "What remains", satisfying the sole-researcher constraint.

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- The functional slot definitions in `morphology.md` §2 are operational enough to steer a tagger without quoting the expected answer.
- Agreement must be computed with the English gloss hidden (tag `local_term` in its source script, or gloss-blind), otherwise agreement is inflated by `gloss_en` leakage — gloss availability is a controlled condition, not a freebie.
- `sources/` (60 sources, ~5 script families per `findings.md` §6, mixed HTML/PDF-extract quality) holds enough raw text for span-level validation.
- Compute is adequate: 225-row classification runs on CPU or as few-shot prompting over an API at dollar cost; Kaggle GPU optional for a linear probe on multilingual embeddings.

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- Row-level κ is high (≈0.8+) for Latin/Cyrillic rows with definitions visible, lower for Arabic/Thai/Han rows.
- Systematic disagreements cluster on the schema's fuzzy boundaries: S4 vs S1, S10 vs S11, S14 vs S10 — giving a ranked list of which slots need sharper definitions.
- Theme codes show materially lower agreement than slots, putting a number on the "keyword coding, no kappa" gap.
- Span-level machine census of `sources/` documents differs from the standards-level census by more than row-level κ would predict — a prescription-versus-practice gap separate from coding noise.

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** Low κ even on Latin-script rows where the definitions are fully visible → the closed vocabulary is not operational enough to bear cross-system claims, and the atlas's figures are one coder's gestalt; alternatively, if machine and hand censuses of documents agree within noise, the second-coder machinery adds no information and the manual code needs no auditing.

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** 225 rows is too few for supervised fine-tuning (few-shot prompting or embedding + linear probe instead); weak multilingual coverage in cheap models for the Arabic/Thai/Han rows; κ measures agreement, not validity — both coders can share one bias, so disagreements must be researcher-audited, which is bounded work (~tens of rows) but not zero.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `requisites.csv` (225 rows, 9 fields) · `morphology.md` §2 (closed slot vocabulary, slot order) · `check_claims.py` (already enforces the closed vocabulary) · `analysis/recompute.py`, `analysis/findings.md` (all figures derived from the hand code) · `README.md` "Honest gaps" (two-coder thematic pass; keyword theme coding, no kappa) · `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md` "What remains" · `sources/` (raw text, script census in `findings.md` §6).

**Not already done (checked).** No tagging/annotation code anywhere in the repo; `recompute.py` regenerates figures from the hand CSV, never re-derives them from text; grep for tagging/annotation/NLP terms matched only prose usages.

## P02-2 — Document-level scale test of the epistolary/normative fork (S6 ⊥ S9)

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Test the perfect complementarity of destination (S6) and preamble (S9) on a corpus of thousands of actual official documents tagged automatically for those two slots, rather than 25 hand-coded systems.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** The atlas calls the fork "the clearest single structural finding", but at system level it rests on 10 informative cells (only-S6 = 8, only-S9 = 2, both = 0, neither = 15) — a strong pattern with a small denominator. Public corpora of official documents (UN documents, EUR-Lex, national gazettes; seeds already sit in `sources/`: `un_res.txt`, `eu_styleguide.txt`, `sn_circulaire.txt`) let a tagger measure the co-occurrence *rate* at document scale with a real denominator: near-zero across genres and scripts hardens the universality claim, a structured nonzero cluster scopes it to subgenres.

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- Official-document corpora are downloadable at scale with no cost or licence barrier (UN/OECD/EUR-Lex/gazette portals are open; assume reachable, verify at fetch time).
- Destination and preamble are detectable in extracted text/layout: address blocks and recital markers ("whereas", 依据, Вاصة, «بما أن», etc.) — a small seed lexicon per script plus model verification is enough for a first pass.
- Document-level tagging generalises from standards to produced documents (this is precisely what is being tested, so the tagger's own accuracy is audited on a hand-checked random subsample).

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- P(S6 ∧ S9) stays low overall (< a few percent) but nonzero, concentrated in appointment/delegation subgenres where a normative act names a person.
- S6-only documents form the correspondence cluster; the fork survives per-tradition breakdown, i.e. genre beats nation at document scale too (replicating `morphology.md` §5 finding 2 with documents instead of systems).
- Variation across traditions is smaller than the genre contrast in the tagged corpus.

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** Co-occurrence rates comparable to the marginals (the fork dissolves at document level → the system-level finding was an artefact of coding standards rather than documents); or detection failure in ≥2 of 5 script families (method falsified, claim untested rather than refuted).

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** Corpus acquisition and text-extraction quality (OCR/HTML boilerplate) dominate the cost; circularity risk if genre is read from document metadata — the test must rest on slot co-occurrence only, never on metadata genre labels; no gold labels at scale, so a manual audit subsample (researcher-only, no participants) is mandatory.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `analysis/findings.md` §2 (both = 0; 8/2/15 split) · `morphology.md` §3, §5 finding 2 · `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §1 (negative implication rules, S9 never with S4/S6/S7/S8/S10/S14) · `sources/` (seed documents) · `diversity.md` §2 (extended-set matrix showing the same fork).

**Not already done (checked).** The fork is computed only from `requisites.csv` system rows; no document corpus, no tagging of document texts, no rates with denominators exist in the repo.

## P02-3 — Where does the genre signal live? Cross-script transfer of genre classifiers

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Train epistolary-vs-normative classifiers on surface (lexical/layout) features in one tradition and on structural (slot-presence) features in another, then measure zero-shot cross-script transfer to decompose "genre, not nation" into deep-grammar versus ritual-surface signal.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** Two headline claims pull in opposite predictive directions: "genre is the main axis" implies genre should be learnable and portable, while "the surface is where traditions diverge" implies a model keyed to surface cues should fail when the tradition and script change. Comparing the transfer gap of a lexical model against a structural model turns that qualitative dichotomy into a measurable decomposition; UN parallel texts (same content in six official translations) give a natural control where content is held constant and only surface changes.

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- Enough labelled documents exist — pilot labels come from the `genre` column of `requisites.csv` plus a researcher hand-labelling of a few hundred documents, or the P02-2 corpus once its tagger is audited (ideas chain but P02-3 has a hand-labelled fallback).
- A multilingual encoder trains within Kaggle's 30 GPU-hours/week (small model, few hundred to a few thousand docs — assume yes; CPU inference for evaluation).
- Parallel UN texts are obtainable in the six official languages (they are published as parallel documents; assume, verify at fetch time).
- Structural features (slot presence/position, block order) are extractable from raw text, not only from layout — assume partial; OCR-failing layouts degrade this arm first.

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- Structural-feature classifiers transfer across scripts/traditions with a small accuracy drop; lexical classifiers lose substantially more — the decomposition the design is built to reveal.
- On parallel texts, accuracy is stable across the six translations for the structural model and varies for the lexical model: genre rides on the skeleton, surface rituals are language-bound.
- The genre signal is recoverable from a handful of slots (S6/S9 plus order), consistent with the fork being near-sufficient.

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** Both feature sets transfer equally well (surface carries genre everywhere → challenges "surface diverges"); or both collapse cross-tradition (genre signal is tradition-bound → challenges "genre, not nation"); or the structural model does no better than majority class even in-domain (slot presence does not encode genre as claimed).

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** Chains on tagged data (P02-2) or hand labels (fallback); class imbalance in public corpora (normative acts may dominate); translationese confound in parallel texts (translations share register conventions) — controlled by reporting native-script-only and parallel results separately.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `morphology.md` §5 finding 2 (genre not nation) and §3 (the fork) · `analysis/findings.md` §4 (clustering collapses to one trunk; sparsity artefacts) · `analysis/findings.md` §6 (script census: Latin/Cyrillic/Arabic/Han/Thai) · `requisites.csv` (`genre` column, per-row hand labels) · `diversity.md` §12c (genre defined by which formulas it drops).

**Not already done (checked).** No classifier, embedding, transfer or parallel-text analysis exists; similarity work is Jaccard on hand-coded presence only (`analysis/recompute.py`).

## P02-4 — Semantic gate: entailment check of every ledger claim against its retrieved source

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Extend `check_claims.py` from structural citation resolution to semantic verification: an LLM entailment pass that checks each of the 92 ledger claims against its retrieved source text, requires a verbatim quote, string-matches the quote into the source, and flags unsupported claims for researcher review.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** The current gate proves that every claim-id and source-id *resolves* — it cannot detect a claim whose wording overstates, drifts from, or contradicts its source, which is the failure mode a citation graph is most exposed to. With 57 of 60 sources already on disk as plain text, each claim can be tested by a cheap model that returns supported / partially supported / not found plus a supporting quote; the quote must then appear verbatim in the source string, which mechanically defeats hallucinated evidence. The output is a ranked review queue, never an automatic edit — the researcher stays the arbiter, so this strengthens the existing gate rather than replacing judgement.

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- Retrieved texts cover their claims' evidence (true for 57 sources; the 4 provisional claims from blocked hosts will correctly return "not found" — an expected, informative result, not a failure).
- Claims are written at a granularity where one span can support them (mostly true of the ledger's source-paired claims; interpretive cross-file claims may legitimately return "partial").
- Cross-lingual entailment works on ~$-level spend: claims are in English, sources in UA/RU/AR/ZH/FR — assume a cheap multilingual-capable model suffices, verify on a manual subsample first.
- Cost: roughly 92–200 model calls; fits "no large budget" trivially (repo's own machine arm cost $0.0314 for 864 calls).

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- ≥90% of the 88 verified claims return "supported" with a quote that string-matches.
- The 4 provisional claims and a small tail of interpretive claims (e.g. ancestor/causal statements in `diversity.md` §13) return partial or not-found, producing a prioritised review queue rather than new failures.
- No verified claim is found flatly contradicted by its source (provenance discipline holds).

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** A large mismatch rate (>10–15% of verified claims unmatchable) → the ledger's prose overstates its sources and the "88 verified" figure needs redefinition; or the pass agrees with manual reading no better than chance on a 15-claim hand-checked subsample → the tool is noise and cannot be wired into the gate.

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** Boilerplate-heavy HTML sources (e.g. wiki dumps in `sources/`) can induce false entailment — mitigated by exact-quote matching and, where needed, section pre-extraction; cross-lingual weakness on cheap models for Arabic/Thai/Ge'ez material — mitigated by the manual subsample validation before wiring anything in.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `ledger.json` (92 claims / 60 sources) · `check_claims.py` (schema, vocabulary, CSV integrity, internal consistency, citation resolution — everything *except* semantics) · `README.md` "The gate" · `sources/` (57 retrieved texts) · the 4 provisional claims (RU/GOST R 7.0.97; UAE guideline index).

**Not already done (checked).** README states the gate's scope explicitly; no entailment, quote-matching or content-verification step exists in `check_claims.py` or elsewhere (repo-wide scan for entailment/verification terms negative).

## P02-5 — Continuous register index: dose–response for the comprehension experiment, plus difficulty calibration

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Replace the two-level formal/plain label with a continuous, automatically computed register index over the stimulus texts (and any content-gated paraphrase ladder), reanalyse the existing machine-arm accuracy as a dose–response, and use the same index plus machine item difficulty to select a harder item pool that breaks the ceiling — logged as a pre-registration revision before any human data.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** `morphology.md` §6 names parsing cost as a missing efficiency metric, while the experiment currently collapses register to a binary with endpoints that turned out equivalent (−0.2 pp, TOST equivalent) and an item set still at ceiling for 6 of 12 readers. Computing per-text indices (sentence length, dependency depth, nominalization/passive rate, frequency profile, embedding distance to a plain-language reference) creates a dose variable over which accuracy can be regressed — a null between two endpoints does not exclude a slope somewhere along the continuum, and the weakest reader (41.7%) is where any slope should appear. Because the human arm has not run, index-based item selection can be folded in as Revision 2 (precedent: Revision 1, which was made pre-collection for exactly the ceiling reason).

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- The register index is computable CPU-side: 24 short stimulus texts with ordinary dependency parsing — trivially yes; a paraphrase ladder of, say, 4–6 levels × 6 obligations stays within a dollar or two of API spend.
- The current formal and plain endpoints separate cleanly on the index (checked first; if they do not, the manipulation itself is weak on the continuous scale — reported as a manipulation failure, per `PREREGISTRATION.md` §conditional).
- Ladder generation preserves content across levels — gated mechanically in the style of `check_stimuli.py` (identical numeric tokens, gold fact tuples unchanged).
- The index and analysis are registered before being run, or the result is a garden of forking paths.

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- Endpoints separate clearly on the index (continuous manipulation check passes).
- Accuracy–index slope ≈ 0 for capable readers but negative for the weak readers — register costs the weak reader, not the strong one — even though the endpoint TOST stays equivalent.
- Machine item difficulty aligns with the known gradient (extraction 0.948 vs inference 0.910), so machine-calibrated hard items restore variance for the human arm.

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** Index cannot separate formal from plain (labels do not correspond to measurable register → the manipulation is invalid, not merely null); or the slope is flat even for weak readers across a wide index range (register irrelevant to machine reading, generalising the endpoint null); or machine-calibrated items still sit at ceiling for human-equivalent difficulty (calibration route fails, and item difficulty needs a different fix).

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** 24 texts are few — the ladder adds constructed stimuli, stacking onto the already-declared limitation that the experimenter wrote all stimuli (`PREREGISTRATION.md` §10.1); any human-facing change requires a logged Revision 2 before the human arm runs, and administering the revised instrument to participants is **ethics review required** (the machine-only reanalysis is not); multiple candidate indices tempt post-hoc selection — one primary index must be pre-specified.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `experiment/stimuli.json` (6 obligations × {en,uk} × {formal,plain}) · `experiment/machine_arm.py`, `experiment/results/RESULTS.md` (−0.2 pp, TOST equivalent at ±10 pp, 6/12 readers at ceiling, weakest at 41.7%) · `experiment/PREREGISTRATION.md` §6–§7, §10 · `experiment/DEVIATIONS.md` Revision 1 (pre-collection revision precedent; Erratum 1 shows the log's discipline) · `morphology.md` §5–§6 (nothing optimised for comprehension; efficiency needs a metric) · `experiment/README.md` "ceiling finding".

**Not already done (checked).** Register is binary everywhere in `experiment/`; no readability/complexity index, no paraphrase ladder, no IRT/difficulty calibration exists (repo scan for paraphrase/difficulty-calibration work negative; DEVIATIONS contains only the three logged entries).

## P02-6 — Slot ablation: the first quantitative per-slot functional value profile

**Participant: P02 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement [idea].** Remove one canonical slot at a time from full official documents (real or template-instantiated) and measure the drop in a machine reader's ability to answer who-did-what-to-whom questions, yielding a per-slot functional-value profile that tests whether the "universal stem" is also the load-bearing core.

**Mechanism/rationale [idea].** `morphology.md` §6 says efficiency would need a metric — parsing cost, ambiguity, error in downstream action — and the atlas has none; finding 5 asserts the slots are for validity, not comprehension, without measuring what any slot does for a reader. Ablation turns the closed vocabulary into an experimental factor: document → minus S3, minus S12, minus S9, minus S4 … → identical extraction/inference questions → accuracy/latency delta per slot, with a sham-deletion control for removal artefacts. The `obligation` column of `requisites.csv` (obligatory vs optional) hands over a ready-made dose prediction: obligatory slots should hurt more when removed than optional ones.

**Assumptions [assumption].**
- Full documents with most slots present can be assembled: real texts exist in `sources/` (`sn_circulaire.txt`, `un_res.txt`, decree/letter examples in case files) and the standards' own templates can be instantiated with content held constant across ablation conditions — both routes assumed workable, verified per document.
- Slot removal is expressible as text deletion for most slots; layout/image-only slots (e.g. an S4 emblem) degrade to placeholder deletion, handled explicitly rather than silently.
- Machine readers are an accepted proxy for a *machine-legibility* functional test — the repo itself scopes arm M exactly that way, so no claim about human comprehension is implied and **no ethics review is required** for the machine-only version.
- Compute: a few hundred to a few thousand calls at the repo's demonstrated cost scale ($0.0314 per 864 calls), CPU-side prompting or Kaggle GPU for a local small model.

**Predicted observations [prediction].**
- Ablating stem slots (S3 origin, S5 temporal, S11 dispositif, S12 authentication) hurts more than ablating surface slots (S4 authority-mark, S8 subject, S7 reference) — the deep grammar is functionally load-bearing.
- S12 ablation specifically hits "who issued / is this binding" items — a functional correlate of its 17/25 universality.
- Obligatory requisites show larger ablation effects than optional ones (`requisites.csv` `obligation` column predicts effect size).
- The hard inference items (Q3-class) separate conditions better than extraction items, mirroring the 0.948/0.910 gradient.

**What would disconfirm it [prediction].** A flat ablation profile (all slots equally dispensable → the deep grammar is descriptive baggage, challenging the framing of `morphology.md` §1/§5); surface slots matter more than stem slots (the divergent surface is what readers rely on → direct challenge to finding 5's "nothing is optimised" reading); or no effect at all with a working sham control (machine readers bypass document structure entirely — consistent with, and astrengthening of, arm M's register null).

**Main feasibility risk [assumption].** Ecological validity of ablated or template-instantiated texts — the sham-deletion control and, where possible, ablation of real documents first, are the guard; the old ceiling problem returns unless the harder Q3-class items (or P02-5's calibrated items) are used from the start; layout-only slots are poorly represented in plain-text extraction, so some slots may be untestable in this design and must be declared out of scope rather than estimated.

**Connects to (repo-verified).** `morphology.md` §2 (slot order = syntax), §5 finding 1 (universal stem), §6 (efficiency metric open) · `analysis/findings.md` §1 (S12 = 17/25; slot census) · `requisites.csv` (`obligation`, `slot`, `genre` columns — the design matrix) · `experiment/machine_arm.py`, `experiment/analysis_core.py`, `experiment/stimuli.json` (Q3 inference items), `experiment/results/RESULTS.md` (ceiling note) — the harness is reusable as-is.

**Not already done (checked).** No ablation, deletion study or per-slot functional measurement exists anywhere in the repo; the experiment manipulates *register* over fixed texts, never *slot presence*.
