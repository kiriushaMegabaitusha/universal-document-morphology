## P03-1 — Measure the cost, not just the error: a pre-registered processing-cost outcome for the comprehension test

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Add a pre-registered processing-cost outcome — per-question response time, plus a time-bounded fact-location ("findability") version of the hardest items — as a co-primary outcome beside accuracy in the finding-5 comprehension test, so the register question can be answered on *cost of understanding* even if accuracy stays at ceiling.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** `morphology.md` §6 says the missing efficiency metric is exactly this: parsing cost and time-to-complete, which no standard measures; the experiment currently operationalises comprehension only as four-option accuracy, an outcome already at ceiling for 9–12 machine readers and suspected of ceiling for humans (`README.md` honest gaps). Register effects in reading typically show up as extra processing time before they show up as errors, so a speed measure can move under a null accuracy result — the dissociation "same answers, more work" would be the first positive comprehension signal in the project either way.

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` The instrument already timestamps trials (`rt_ms`, `PREREGISTRATION.md` §5, `build_survey.py`) with enough granularity to compare conditions — to be verified; if it is per-document rather than per-answer, a small **pre-data** instrument revision under the established `DEVIATIONS.md` discipline suffices. `[assumption]` Participants are not speeded in a way that changes accuracy (no speed–accuracy tradeoff masking the contrast). `[assumption]` The instrument's line "There is no time limit and no 'right' speed" would need rewriting for a findability block — a participant-facing change that must happen before any human data.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` Formal > plain median time-to-correct-answer, with the gap larger on inference items than extraction items (register × kind interaction in RT); accuracy unchanged. `[prediction]` On a time-bounded fact-location task, plain renderings yield more correct locations within the deadline. `[prediction]` A null RT contrast with a CI excluding a difference of, say, ≥10% of median RT would instead strengthen a "register is cognitively cheap for these items" reading.

**What would disconfirm it.** Plain slower than formal on correct trials with a CI excluding zero in the other direction; or any RT gap fully explained by fast errors (no cost on correct answers); or an equivalence CI on cost that is narrow around zero under both the open and the time-bounded version.

**Main feasibility risk.** RT in a browser conflates reading, deciding and distraction; a per-document timestamp would be too coarse. Fix = per-question timing (code only) + pre-registered trimming of outliers, `simulate_data.py` re-run for power on the RT outcome. Machine-arm `latency_ms` exists as a free manipulation check but measures model speed, not human comprehension.

**Ethics:** **ethics review required** for the confirmatory version (new human participants; the machine-only pilot and the timing code change need none).

**Connects to.** `morphology.md` §5–6 (finding 5, the missing efficiency metric); `experiment/PREREGISTRATION.md` §5 (`rt_ms` recorded, currently descriptive only), §6 (only accuracy is registered), §7 (`simulate_data.py` power tool); `experiment/README.md` (ceiling warning); `experiment/results/` (no latency analysis exists — verified).

## P03-2 — The declension as a cognitive variable: does the epistolary/normative fork change comprehension of an identical obligation?

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Re-render the six experimental obligations in both declensions — as a letter (destination + reference, no preamble) and as a normative act (preamble, no addressee) — holding register, language and fact tuples fixed, and measure whether the fork's furniture (S6 ⊥ S9) helps or hinders readers finding and acting on the same obligation.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** The fork is the atlas's clearest structural fact (`analysis/findings.md` §2: both = 0 across 25 systems; `morphology.md` §3) yet the experiment holds document morphology at **zero** furniture — `stimuli.json` documents are bare prose passages ("12 short passages", `build_survey.py`). If the declension exists only for validity and procedure, adding or removing its furniture should be cognitively inert (supporting finding 5); if the preamble delays the action line or the addressee orients the reader, the fork acquires a measurable reader-side consequence — a direct test of whether the deep grammar is legally real but cognitively inert.

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` The same gold fact tuples can be re-rendered into both declensions with no content change — `check_stimuli.py`'s content-preservation and answer-key machinery extends to two more variants per obligation. `[assumption]` Register can be crossed with genre (2 × 2) without doubling session length — otherwise run one register first. `[assumption]` Participants' prior experience with one genre (students are used to rules, not chancery letters) is a nuisance covariate, not a confound of the furniture itself.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` A reader-role × declension fit: obligations phrased as rules located/acted on faster under normative furniture, obligations directed at "you" handled faster under epistolary furniture, with a register × genre interaction smaller than the genre main effect. `[prediction]` If the fork is inert, furniture effects are near zero while an "who is this addressed to?" manipulation check still shows the furniture was noticed.

**What would disconfirm it.** No furniture effect above a pre-set bound (re-express the SESOI for accuracy; set a bound on RT) in either direction with tight CIs; or an effect that disappears once perceived addressee/authority is controlled (meaning it was demand, not morphology); or content-preservation checks failing (test invalid rather than hypothesis falsified).

**Main feasibility risk.** Stimulus authoring multiplies (6 obligations × 2 declensions × registers × languages) and screen count must stay near 12 to protect recruitment and power; machine-arm pre-testing is ~$0.03 but sits near ceiling, so it can validate parsing, not the effect.

**Ethics:** **ethics review required** for the human version (new participants); no ethics needed for the machine-arm validation.

**Connects to.** `morphology.md` §3 (two declensions) and §5 finding 2 (genre > nation); `analysis/findings.md` §2 and `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §1 (negative rule S9 ⊥ S4/S6/S7/S8/S10/S14); `experiment/stimuli.json` + `check_stimuli.py` (variant machinery); `experiment/build_survey.py` (rendering pipeline). **Not already done:** no genre/declension factor exists anywhere in `experiment/` (grep verified).

## P03-3 — Is the canonical slot order a reader aid? Intact vs shuffled layout on fact memory and location

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Present identical document content once in the atlas's canonical slot order (identity → origin → date → body → authentication → annex) and once with the self-contained blocks shuffled, then probe memory for and source-location of slot-specific facts (identifier, date, authentication) — testing whether "the ordering is the *syntax* of the document" (`morphology.md` §2) is a cognitive asset or only a legal one.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** The atlas's two live explanations for the deep grammar are "it encodes what law requires" and "it is a universal structure", but nothing in the repo asks whether readers exploit the order; finding 5 says nothing in the formats is optimised for comprehension. An intact-vs-scrambled manipulation with content held constant pits **deep grammar as reader-functional** against **deep grammar as validity-only** — the exact deep-grammar-vs-tradition discrimination the evaluation criteria name as the top information-gain target.

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` Whole-block relocation keeps every sentence self-contained (no dangling anaphora) — otherwise scrambling changes readability, not order, and the test is confounded. `[assumption]` Probes must be closed-book (document hidden after study) or time-limited, because open-book lookups make order irrelevant by design. `[assumption]` Study time is fixed per screen so faster reading of one layout is not mistaken for better memory.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` Intact order → faster and more accurate *location* of S2/S5/S12 facts and better cross-slot integration questions ("who issued it, and when"), smallest or no gain on single-line lookups. `[prediction]` If the effect only appears under a short study window, order is a processing aid that vanishes with unlimited time — still informative. `[prediction]` No difference with tight CIs = order is legally real but cognitively inert, which *supports* finding 5 and is a publishable null under the repo's own decision-rule discipline.

**What would disconfirm it.** Scrambled ≥ intact on both accuracy and location latency with CIs excluding a meaningful intact advantage; or an effect that vanishes when content-preserving scrambling is verified (i.e., it was anaphora damage, not order); or failure of the manipulation check (readers cannot report a difference in layout at all).

**Main feasibility risk.** Writing scramble variants that stay propositionally identical is fiddly and error-prone; `check_stimuli.py`-style gating must be extended to assert block-multiset equality. Sample splitting across conditions costs power — re-run `simulate_data.py` before committing to a design.

**Ethics:** **ethics review required** for the human version (new participants). A no-ethics machine variant exists: give the model one layout, then hide it and ask a memory probe (closed-book) — cheap, with the explicit caveat that machine memory ≠ human memory.

**Connects to.** `morphology.md` §2 (order = syntax) and §5 findings 1 & 5; `analysis/findings.md` §1 (slot census, S12 = 17/25); `experiment/build_survey.py` + `stimuli.json` (rendering pipeline to add layout variants); `brainstorm/CRITERIA.md` (deep-grammar-vs-tradition named as the exemplar information-gain target). **Not already done:** no layout/order manipulation exists in the repo (grep verified).

## P03-4 — Stress test the null: does a register effect emerge under time pressure or concurrent load?

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Pre-specify, before any human data, a stress condition — bounded display time or a light concurrent memory load — and register a register × load interaction, so the human arm cannot fail merely by being too easy, and so a null at leisure is not over-read as equivalence under load.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** Both arms now threaten ceiling (`experiment/README.md`: 6 of 12 machine readers at 100%; `README.md` honest gaps: "this item set may be too easy for the intended human readers"), and the machine null is explicitly non-diagnostic. If plain register's hypothesised benefit is a *cost saving* rather than an accuracy gain, savings are invisible when resources are unlimited and visible when they are scarce — so a stress arm converts an uninformative null into either a moderated effect (register matters when reading is hard) or a much stronger equivalence claim (no gap even under load).

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` A bounded display or concurrent load does not differentially damage the formal condition *except through* register — checkable via the manipulation check and a load-manipulation check (participants report the load). `[assumption]` The stress condition can be added without doubling session length (between-screen randomisation, or a between-participant arm), and N = 36 remains adequate — must be re-verified in `simulate_data.py`, since splitting cells costs power. `[assumption]` Floor effects are avoidable: display duration has to be pilot-set so plain-rendering accuracy is not itself crushed.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` Register gap ≈ 0 at leisure but ≥ SESOI (10 pp) under stress, pre-specified as a register × load interaction coefficient in `logit_fe`. `[prediction]` Machine analogue: reducing the reader's token budget or prefixing a distractor makes formal renderings relatively worse — an exploratory, no-ethics precursor run costing ~$0.03. `[prediction]` No interaction with a CI excluding a 10 pp moderation would upgrade the current "no detectable effect" to "robustly absent under stress too".

**What would disconfirm it.** Interaction in the opposite direction (formal improved under load); no interaction with a CI excluding the pre-set moderation bound; or the load failing its own manipulation check (then the test is uninformative rather than negative).

**Main feasibility risk.** Load manipulations are easy to get wrong (floor effects, participants ignoring the secondary task), and any post-data change would break the pre-registration's credibility — hence the whole design must be a **Revision 2** logged like Revision 1 *before* human data exist. Session-length creep hurts recruitment.

**Ethics:** **ethics review required** for the human version (new participants); machine-arm precursor needs none.

**Connects to.** `experiment/PREREGISTRATION.md` §7 (power at SESOI, design B) and §8 (contingencies); `experiment/DEVIATIONS.md` Revision 1 (the pre-data-change precedent); `README.md` honest gaps + `experiment/README.md` (ceiling); `analysis_core.py` `logit_fe` (the interaction slot already exists — H2/H3 use it). **Not already done:** no load or time-pressure factor exists in the repo (grep verified).

## P03-5 — Does the validity layer reach the reader? Authentication block → trust, willingness to act, comprehension

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Manipulate the authentication block (signed-and-sealed vs "signature withheld" vs absent) on otherwise identical obligations and measure three separable outcomes — trust in the document, willingness to comply, and fact comprehension — to test whether the atlas's most universal slot does any work in a reader's head.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** Authentication is the most universal slot (17/25, `analysis/findings.md` §1) and the most variable in form (`diversity.md` §3), and the atlas's causal story is that formats encode *validity*; `morphology.md` §5 calls the gap between "legally valid" and "actually understood" the real structural inefficiency, but no repo component measures the reader's side of that gap. A dissociation — authentication moves trust/compliance intent but not comprehension — would empirically separate the validity layer from the comprehension layer; no movement at all would say the state's validity signals do not reach citizens cognitively, which threatens an assumption the atlas leans on.

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` Trust and compliance intent can be captured by one or two ratings per screen without turning the instrument into an attitude survey (session-length budget). `[assumption]` The manipulation is salient — it must be (manipulation check: "who signed this?"). `[assumption]` Obligation content, register and layout are byte-identical across authentication variants, so only S12 varies.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` Signature/seal present → higher stated trust and willingness to comply, unchanged extraction/inference accuracy (validity layer psychologically real, comprehension layer untouched). `[prediction]` Interaction with register: authentication rescues willingness to comply for formal renderings more than plain ones. `[prediction]` Machine precursor: model willingness to *follow* the document's instruction versus a competing instruction shifts when an authentication block is present — a no-ethics proxy for authority sensitivity.

**What would disconfirm it.** No trust/compliance movement with tight CIs (readers ignore the validity layer entirely); or accuracy effects fully mediating trust (then it is engagement, not authority); or the manipulation failing its salience check.

**Main feasibility risk.** Self-report trust is demand-sensitive and conditions are visible — the same limitation already declared at `PREREGISTRATION.md` §10.2 — and adding a third outcome lengthens the session, cutting power per euro. Mitigation: within-item design with one rating, order randomised as in the current instrument.

**Ethics:** **ethics review required** for the human version (new participants); the machine precursor needs none.

**Connects to.** Finding 1 (authentication, 17/25) in `README.md`/`analysis/findings.md` §1; `diversity.md` §3 (most variable slot) and §10b (trace/access/appraisal regimes); `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §2 (authentication = top theme, 17 files); `morphology.md` §5 (validity vs understanding); `experiment/stimuli.json` (currently has no signature block — the obligation text merely *mentions* a signature in one item). **Not already done:** authentication is an outcome-relevant manipulated factor nowhere in the repo (grep verified).

## P03-6 — Formality in the corpus, not in the experimenter's head: a surface-complexity index over `sources/`

**Participant: P03 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15**

**Statement (idea).** Build a CPU-only, regenerable surface-formality index (sentence length, subordination depth, nominalisation and passive rates, direct-address and directive markers) over the 69 extracted source texts in `sources/`, join it to system and tradition via `ledger.json`, and use it to (a) validate that the experiment's constructed `en-formal`/`uk-formal` renderings sit inside the corpus's actual formal range and (b) ask whether formality differs across the five kinds of document law while slot structure converges.

**Mechanism / rationale (idea).** The experiment's declared limitation #1 is that the experimenter wrote *both* renderings (`PREREGISTRATION.md` §10.1), so nobody knows whether the "formal" stimuli are as formal as real official texts; meanwhile finding 5 claims no standard optimises comprehension, yet formality is never measured anywhere in the repo (theme coding is keyword-based — the repo's own honest gaps). A corpus index turns register from a binary authored manipulation into a continuous, externally anchored variable — instrument design with zero recruitment, and a new between-system measurable that can be rerun by `recompute.py`-style tooling whenever sources are added.

**Assumptions.** `[assumption]` Extraction quality of the 69 `.txt` files is good enough for surface metrics (per-file noise audit first; some PDFs extract poorly). `[assumption]` Language-appropriate tokenisation is available on CPU for at least the two largest script families (Latin 120 / Cyrillic 56 cells, per `analysis/findings.md` §6); Han and Thai metrics are deferred to a later, within-script phase rather than compared across scripts. `[assumption]` Source → system → tradition joins are recoverable from `ledger.json` keys and filenames without inventing mappings.

**Predicted observations.** `[prediction]` A single dominant factor separates plain-language/guidance texts from statutory/standard texts *within* the same system (sanity pass), and the constructed `formal` stimuli fall in the corpus's upper formality quantile in both languages. `[prediction]` Between-system variance is smaller than within-system genre variance — the wording-level echo of finding 2 (genre > nation). `[prediction]` If kinds-of-document-law predict formality (e.g., formula-heavy Francophone and function-heavy Soviet texts clustering apart at the surface), finding 5 gains its first quantitative object.

**What would disconfirm it.** The index fails its sanity check (cannot separate the repo's own plain-language/guidance sources from statutory texts) or metrics disagree among themselves (index unreliable) — tool invalid, abandon or rework. Stimuli landing mid-distribution would disconfirm the *validation* assumption and instead trigger a stimuli calibration — a useful correction, not a dead end.

**Main feasibility risk.** Multilingual surface metrics: no spaces in Han, different morphology in Cyrillic → cross-script comparisons are invalid; mitigation is a strictly within-script, per-file-distribution reporting scheme first. Secondary risk: join ambiguity between sources and systems (some sources cover several systems) — resolved by treating source-level, not system-level, values as primary.

**Ethics:** not applicable — no human participants.

**Connects to.** `sources/` (69 `.txt` extracted texts, 64 MB) and `ledger.json` (60 sources, keys for the join); `analysis/findings.md` §5–6 (evidence base, script families) and §7 (caveats); `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §6 (keyword coding caveat); `STANDARDS.md` (layers give known-formal vs known-plain anchors, incl. the plain-language layer); `morphology.md` §5–6 (finding 5, the missing efficiency metric); `experiment/PREREGISTRATION.md` §10.1 + `stimuli.json` `register_conventions` (stimulus-validation target); `analysis/recompute.py` (the regeneration/gate pattern a new derived figure should follow). **Not already done:** no readability, complexity or formality metric exists anywhere in `analysis/` or `experiment/` (read both; grep verified).
