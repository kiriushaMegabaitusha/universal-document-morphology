## P05-1 — Trace density as a measurable state-capacity indicator

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Convert the atlas's qualitative claim that trace/audit density (S14) tracks the strength of the central recordkeeping regime into a quantitative per-system index derived from `requisites.csv`, and test whether it covaries with an externally compiled cross-national indicator of digital-government or public-administration capacity.
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** `analysis/findings.md` §1 and §3 already compute per-system slot counts (e.g., S14 present in 11 of 25 slot-coded systems; Ukraine devotes 8 of 32 requisites to trace, DIN 5008 essentially none), but the "regime strength" label behind the claim is hand-assigned and, per `README.md` "Honest gaps", not derivable from the CSVs. An external index supplies an independent measurement of the thing the trace claim purports to track, turning a narrative correlation into a falsifiable one; if it fails, the honest revision is that S14 density marks *standard type* (complete state делопроизводство system vs private-sector page standard), not state capacity.
- **[assumption]** A free, comparable cross-national indicator of digital-government development or public-administration quality exists for most of the 25 slot-coded systems (candidate indicators to be identified in a later, literature-allowed round; none named here).
- **[assumption]** S14 density (trace requisites / total requisites, computable from `requisites.csv`) is a valid proxy for recordkeeping-regime strength; measurement error is concentrated in the encyclopedia-thin cases flagged in `analysis/evidence_base.csv`.
- **[assumption]** Unit alignment is solvable: traditions and supranational bodies in the corpus (Francophone tradition, Soviet bloc, Islamicate chancery, UN, EU, khipu, wampum) have no country index value and must be declared exclusions in advance, leaving roughly N = 15–20.
- **[prediction]** S14 density ranks positively with the external indicator across the analysable systems.
- **[prediction]** The association survives leave-one-out removal of the densest (SU, UA) and the thinnest-coded cases.
- **[prediction]** Within the 13 "page-format standards" systems the association attenuates toward zero — the honest secondary prediction if the real driver is standard type rather than capacity.
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** A correlation near zero (or wrong-signed) with a confidence interval excluding a policy-relevant effect size; or an association that vanishes once standard-type/regime class is entered as a control, showing the original finding was a category label in circular clothing.
- **Main feasibility risk.** N after exclusions is small and units are heterogeneous (systems vs countries), so the test can be underpowered and ecologically crude; hand-assigned regime classes mean the control variable reproduces the same subjectivity the test is meant to check.
- **Repo connections.** `morphology.md` §5 finding 3 (trace density ↔ recordkeeping regime); `analysis/findings.md` §1 (slot frequency) and §3 (coding density by regime class); `analysis/slot_matrix.csv`; `analysis/evidence_base.csv` (grounding tiers as measurement-error map); `README.md` honest gaps (hand-assigned regime classes); `analysis/recompute.py` (the figure-derivation gate this new index should be bolted onto).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (secondary data, public documents only).

## P05-2 — A within-system digitisation trajectory: do slots migrate into infrastructure?

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Reconstruct the pre- and post-digitisation slot profiles of one Ukrainian document flow (the ГОСТ 6.38 → ДСТУ 4163 official-documentation lineage plus the electronic document-management rules) to test `diversity.md` §7's claim that "the medium moves the slots into infrastructure" on an observed reform trajectory instead of a single endpoint comparison.
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** §7 infers slot migration cross-sectionally — Estonia as the far endpoint, Brazil's Sidof one generation earlier — with no before/after inside one system. Ukraine offers a dated, publicly documented sequence of documentation regimes (Soviet inheritance → ДСТУ 4163:2020 → electronic document flow), so one can code each successive standard with the same closed 14-slot vocabulary and observe whether the *carriers* change while the *slots* persist.
- **[assumption]** Full texts of the earlier and later standard/resolution documents are retrievable (some Ukrainian and Soviet-era texts may be paywalled or on unstable portals; the RU/GOST precedent in `cases/ru.md` shows this risk is real).
- **[assumption]** Slot presence can be validly coded from standard texts plus published specimens, without needing a live document archive.
- **[assumption]** Reform dates are sharp enough for pre/post bins — a stronger version of the approximate anchor-year dating that `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 already declares coarse.
- **[prediction]** Slot invariance: none of the universal stem slots (S1, S3, S5, S10/S11, S12) disappears from the standard text after digitisation.
- **[prediction]** S14 requisites shift carrier — overt paper marks (registration stamps, execution/acquaintance lines) decline or are re-described as system logs, consistent with `diversity.md` §7's "trace becomes an automatic log".
- **[prediction]** Authentication (S12) changes *form* (hand → qualified electronic signature) while remaining present, matching the finding that S12 is the most universal slot yet the most variable in form.
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** Stem slots drop out of the requisites list after digitisation, or the requisites list is copied forward unchanged with no carrier shift at all — the first would break universality under digitisation, the second would show the standard is inert to medium and §7's mechanism is wrong.
- **Main feasibility risk.** Text retrieval of older Ukrainian/Soviet standard texts; a single country, single flow is one trajectory and cannot by itself support a general claim — it must be positioned as a mechanism-revealing case, not an effect estimate.
- **Repo connections.** `diversity.md` §7 (medium moves slots into infrastructure) and §8; `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §4 (diachrony, coarse bins); `cases/ua.md` and `cases/soviet_bloc.md`; `morphology.md` §1 (ЕГСД as canonicalisation of a record type); `README.md` honest gaps (approximate regime-anchor years).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (standard texts and public documents only).

## P05-3 — Does the S6 ⊥ S9 fork hold at document level, out of sample?

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Test whether the epistolary/normative complementarity (destination never co-occurs with preamble) survives at the level of individual documents in a held-out corpus coded by an automated slot detector, rather than only as an aggregated property of 25 hand-coded systems.
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** `analysis/findings.md` §2 reports the negation over *system-level* cells (both = 0, only-S6 = 8, only-S9 = 2, neither = 15); each cell pools many document types, so individual hybrid documents could violate the rule without moving the system-level count — the "clearest single structural finding" (`morphology.md` §3) has never been stressed at the grain it claims to describe. A held-out document sample with rule-based detection of address blocks vs recitals, validated on a hand-labelled subsample, yields a document-level estimate of P(S9|S6) with a confidence interval.
- **[assumption]** Rule-based detection of an address block (S6) and a recital/whereas preamble (S9) reaches acceptable accuracy — this must itself be measured on a hand-labelled subsample (researcher labelling of public documents; no human subjects → no ethics review).
- **[assumption]** Held-out documents are genuinely independent of those used to build `requisites.csv`; texts in `sources/` may partly overlap and must be treated as a non-independent stratum or excluded.
- **[assumption]** Each sampled document can be classified as letter-like or act-like for stratification, at least by the researcher's reading.
- **[prediction]** Zero or near-zero co-occurrence at document level, with the upper 95% bound below a pre-declared small threshold (e.g., 2%).
- **[prediction]** Any violations concentrate in hybrid genres (orders addressed to a named body, circulars with recitals) rather than spreading across the corpus.
- **[prediction]** Detector accuracy on the hand-labelled subsample ≥ ~0.9 — otherwise the test measures the detector, not the fork (pre-declared as a validity gate in the style of `experiment/check_stimuli.py`).
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** Material document-level co-occurrence above zero with a tight confidence interval — the system-level negation would then be an aggregation artefact (pooling of two clean document populations), which would not destroy the declension model but would restate it as a tendency rather than a formal negation.
- **Main feasibility risk.** Detector accuracy under class imbalance (many documents are neither clean letter nor clean act), plus single-coder labelling with no inter-rater check — the same limitation `README.md` already flags for keyword theme coding; PDF/OCR quality in `sources/` is uneven.
- **Repo connections.** `morphology.md` §3 (two declensions, complementary distribution); `analysis/findings.md` §2 (perfect complementary distribution, S9 co-occurrence profile); `analysis/slot_matrix.csv`; `sources/` (candidate corpus); `experiment/check_stimuli.py` (existing pattern for a mechanical validity gate to mirror).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (public documents; researcher is the only labeller). A later variant using external labellers would require ethics review.

## P05-4 — Genre vs nation, formally: a variance decomposition of slot structure

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Formally test the headline claim "genre, not nation, is the main axis of variation" by partitioning document-level slot-vector variation between genre, country/tradition and their interaction in a small crossed corpus (the same two genres observed in four to six countries).
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** The genre-first ranking is asserted from qualitative comparison (`morphology.md` §5 finding 2, `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY`) and supported negatively by `analysis/findings.md` §4 ("clustering collapses to one trunk, no discrete families"), but no analysis ever partitions the variation — the headline is a reasoned reading, not a measured ordering. A crossed design (2 genres × ~5 countries × ~10 documents each) coded for the 14 slots gives variance shares whose ordering is directly comparable, and whose fragility to dropping any one country can be checked.
- **[assumption]** A crossed corpus of roughly 100 public documents is collectable by one researcher, with both genres documented in every chosen country (some traditions in the corpus are coded for only one genre or are regimes rather than page documents — selection must be declared).
- **[assumption]** Per-document slot coding is reliable enough with one coder plus a detector checked on a subsample; no inter-rater kappa is possible, mirroring the repo's own declared theme-coding caveat.
- **[assumption]** The country set must contain at least one shared-tradition pair (e.g., UA/DE or UA/FR) and one outsider (e.g., CN) so that genre and nation are genuinely contrastive rather than confounded.
- **[assumption]** Binary slot vectors suit the chosen partition method (e.g., a distance-based permutation partition rather than Gaussian ANOVA) — method choice flagged, not fixed, this round.
- **[prediction]** Genre explains more dispersion than country/tradition, and the ordering survives leave-one-country-out.
- **[prediction]** The genre × country interaction is small relative to the genre main effect.
- **[prediction]** Within-genre cross-country similarity exceeds within-country cross-genre similarity on the same Jaccard metric already used in `analysis/slot_similarity.csv`.
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** Country or tradition share ≥ genre share; or the genre effect disappears when the supranational documents (UN/EU) are dropped — the headline would then need rescoping to "genre is the main axis within Western and post-Soviet traditions", which is a productive challenge rather than a failure.
- **Main feasibility risk.** Crossing the design is the bottleneck: finding both genres, retrievable and comparable, in traditions where the corpus itself is thin (evidence tiers in `analysis/evidence_base.csv`); small n per cell makes the partition sensitive to coding errors.
- **Repo connections.** `morphology.md` §5 finding 2; `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY` headline findings (genre axis); `analysis/findings.md` §4 (similarity structure) and `analysis/slot_similarity.csv`; `requisites.csv` (existing system-level coding as the prior); `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §6 (method caveats to inherit).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (public documents only).

## P05-5 — Structure, not register: slot completeness as a predictor of machine legibility on real documents

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Move the machine arm from constructed register stimuli to real public documents and test whether requisite completeness — how many of a system's expected slots an individual document actually carries — predicts how accurately reader models extract its key facts, with register entered as a competing predictor.
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** `experiment/` manipulated *wording* (formal vs plain) and hit a ceiling, so it cannot speak to the atlas's own cybernetic claim that the document is a control record whose fixed slots make it re-constitutable and machine-operable (`morphology.md` §1). Documents that omit expected slots should be harder to parse into a fact tuple regardless of register; sampling real documents across a completeness gradient tests structure directly and re-uses the arm's cheap, resumable runner.
- **[assumption]** Gold fact tuples can be constructed for each sampled real document by the researcher (public documents; no participants → no ethics review), and are checkable because facts are verifiable against the document itself.
- **[assumption]** Real documents show enough variation in slot completeness to avoid repeating the ceiling finding — a pre-run item-difficulty diagnostic is required before committing (the repo's own lesson from v1 → v2 of the stimuli).
- **[assumption]** Compute budget suffices: the existing arm cost $0.0314 for 12 models × 864 administrations; longer real documents raise cost and latency but not order of magnitude (Kaggle free GPU available for a local small-model panel if API access is off).
- **[assumption]** Completeness is not simply document length/complexity in disguise — length, genre and source system must be entered as controls.
- **[prediction]** Extraction accuracy rises with slot completeness after controls.
- **[prediction]** The register effect stays null once structure is controlled, replicating the arm's exploratory finding on a new document class.
- **[prediction]** Weaker reader models gain more from completeness than stronger ones — structure compensates for reader capacity.
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** Completeness has no effect with a tight interval while register or plain difficulty does — the "slots = machine-operability" reading would fail, and the atlas's efficiency claim would lose its machine-facing support.
- **Main feasibility risk.** A second ceiling if documents are easy; confounding between completeness and complexity; the labour bottleneck is pre-keying gold facts per document; single-coder keys with no independent verification.
- **Repo connections.** `experiment/README.md` (ceiling finding: 6 of 12 models at 100%; "the wrong instrument at this difficulty"); `experiment/PREREGISTRATION.md` §10.6 (machine arm measures machine legibility, not comprehension); `experiment/machine_arm.py`, `experiment/analyze.py` (reusable runner and analysis); `morphology.md` §1 (serialised control record) and §5 finding 5 (nothing optimised for comprehension); `STANDARDS.md` Layers 0–1 (layout standards as the completeness-generating layer).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (public documents, no human subjects). Any later human comparison arm would require ethics review.

## P05-6 — Who fills the slot? Document grammar as a measurable administrative burden

Participant: P05 | Stage: independent | Origin: AI-generated | Date: 2026-10-15

- **[idea] Statement.** Add a new column over `requisites.csv` — *who supplies this requisite, the citizen or the administration* — and use it to compute, for one common public service in two or three atlas systems (Ukraine, Estonia, Germany), a slot-level "citizen-supplied requisites" count as a grammar-based pilot measure of administrative burden.
- **[idea] Mechanism / rationale.** `morphology.md` §6 states plainly that efficiency is measured nowhere and that the administrative-burden literature it names studies procedures, not the document grammar — the atlas codes what a document *contains* but never who *produces* each requisite, which is precisely the burden question. `diversity.md` §7's once-only principle predicts that digitised systems move requisites from citizen to backend and dissolve whole shuttle genres; coding "citizen-supplied vs derived" per slot turns that narrative into a countable, cross-nationally comparable quantity that existing burden measures do not provide.
- **[assumption]** The same class of service (e.g., obtaining a standard certificate or registering a routine entitlement) can be identified and its input forms retrieved in all chosen systems via public service portals — retrieval, not analysis, is the main uncertainty.
- **[assumption]** "Citizen-supplied vs administration-derived" can be coded reliably by one researcher from the form and its published legal description; ambiguous cases are declared rather than forced.
- **[assumption]** One service per country is a legitimate *pilot* (explicitly not an estimate of national burden) — generalisation is deferred, not claimed.
- **[assumption]** No ethics review is needed while only artifacts are studied; **flag: any later variant that includes user testing with citizens or interviews with officials is 'ethics review required'.**
- **[prediction]** Estonia shows the fewest citizen-supplied slots (once-only principle operationalised in form design), Ukraine an intermediate count with many identity/address requisites derivable from the register, Germany the most re-supplied fields.
- **[prediction]** Citizen-supplied slots concentrate in identity, destination/address and temporal slots (S1, S3, S5, S6), while identifier, authority-mark, trace and authentication are administration-assigned in digitised systems.
- **[prediction]** Digitisation eliminates entire shuttle genres rather than relabelling slots, so the reduction appears as fewer documents demanded, not merely fewer fields per document.
- **Disconfirmation [prediction].** Citizen-supplied counts are equal across systems; or digitisation merely shifts *which* slot the citizen fills with no net reduction — either result would show the grammar-level construct has no discriminatory power and would, at minimum, productively challenge the once-only narrative in `diversity.md` §7.
- **Main feasibility risk.** Forms change without notice and some portals are login- or geo-restricted (Estonian services may only be fully visible to authenticated users); cross-national non-comparability of the legal entitlement behind "the same service"; single-service-per-country means illustrative, not generalisable, output.
- **Repo connections.** `morphology.md` §6 ("what efficient could even mean"; burden literature named as measuring procedures, not grammar); `diversity.md` §7 (once-only principle dissolving genres; Estonia endpoint); `requisites.csv` (the column to extend, 225 rows); `STANDARDS.md` Layer 2 (records-management standards that mandate backend derivation); `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY` (three regime dimensions — access and persistence — as sibling scalar extensions).
- **Ethics:** not required as designed (public forms only) — **'ethics review required' if human participants or officials are added.**
