## P08-1 — A medium-neutral record grammar, applied to the boundary cases

**Statement (idea).** Replace the "n/a" slot profiles currently assigned to the khipu and the wampum with a second, medium-neutral *record* grammar (abstract slots: provenance, custody, temporal reference, content encoding, authentication/validation, transmission-of-authority), and re-code both boundary systems — plus one further non-documentary record system chosen for source availability — to test whether the atlas's deep grammar is a property of *authoritative records* or specifically of *writing*.

**Mechanism / rationale.** `cases/pe.md` argues the 14 slots presuppose a bounded page with linear reading order, so the khipu is declared out of domain and its profile left empty; but `morphology.md` §1 defines the document functionally (a device for reconstituting an action for absent readers), and that definition is medium-neutral. Recoding the boundary cases against the functional definition, rather than against the page, converts the current "scoped by assertion" into "scoped by test": either the abstract slots fill in cords and belts (universality extends past writing) or they do not (the boundary is real, not definitional).

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* the functional premise of `morphology.md` §1 (records reconstitute action across space/time) applies to non-written media.
- *Assumption:* existing grounding is adequate for presence/absence coding — both boundary systems currently rest on encyclopedia-grade sources only (`analysis/evidence_base.csv`: S-QUIPU, S-WAMPUM, S-WAMPUM-2ROW); deeper retrieval would strengthen but is not required for a first test.
- *Idea:* the third system (e.g. a further Andean/Island-Pacific or message-stick record tradition) is selected on source availability, not on a claim of representativeness.

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* ≥4 of the six abstract slots fill in both the khipu and the wampum (shared abstract core).
- *Prediction 2:* authentication migrates: wampum authority sits in belt *plus* recitation (performance), khipu authority in cord/colour encoding — neither in a page-bound mark.
- *Prediction 3:* the written 14-slot profile cannot be reconstructed from the abstract slots — i.e. the two grammars overlap but are not the same grammar (keeps the atlas's scoped claim intact even if extended).

**What would disconfirm it.** Either boundary system fills ≤1 abstract slot (the grammar does not transfer, so the boundary was an analyst's definitional choice, not a finding); or, when the abstract grammar is applied to the 25 written systems, it fails to recover the universal stem (no common core above the medium).

**Main feasibility risk.** Source depth: both boundary cases are thinly grounded (encyclopedia only), and `requisites.csv` carries just 3 rows each for PE-khipu and HN-wampum; single-coder judgement with no inter-rater check (the repo's own declared caveat for keyword coding).

**Repo connections.** diversity.md §5 and §12e · cases/pe.md, cases/wampum.md · morphology.md §1 · requisites.csv rows for `PE,Inka khipu` and `HN,Haudenosaunee wampum` · README key finding 6 · analysis/theme_matrix.csv "non-documentary boundary = 2".

**Ethics status.** Not applicable — desk-based; no human participants.

## P08-2 — Sovereignty/stewardship as a fourth regime dimension of document law

**Statement (idea).** Test whether Indigenous and community data-sovereignty frameworks constitute a fourth regime dimension of "document law" — **stewardship** (who may use a record, for what purpose, under whose interpretation) — distinct from the atlas's trace, access and persistence/appraisal dimensions.

**Mechanism / rationale.** The catalogue in `EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md` and diversity.md §10b/§12d fixes three policy layers beyond page morphology: trace (Thailand, Ukraine), access (Sweden, Kenya), persistence/appraisal (Nigeria) — all legislated by *states*. Frameworks by which communities attach conditions of use, interpretation and benefit to records they hold (named candidates, **assumption, unverified this round**: First Nations OCAP®-style principles; the CARE principles for Indigenous data governance) regulate records rather than pages, and regulate *use* rather than *reading*. If clauses from such frameworks can be coded with the repo's own method (clause → dimension mapping, as `requisites.csv` does for requisites), the "kinds of document law" catalogue gains a dimension in which the legislator is not the state — a genuine de-centring move.

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* such frameworks exist as retrievable public texts (must be verified by search in a later round; not searched now).
- *Assumption:* their clauses are not already reducible to the existing access dimension (access governs *who may read*; stewardship governs *who may use and how*).
- *Assumption:* a solo non-community researcher may code community governance texts as data without community validation — the weakest point of the idea (see risk).

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* a clause-level pass yields obligations with no fit under trace, access or persistence (residual category > 0).
- *Prediction 2:* these regimes attach to record systems/fonds rather than to documents — unit of governance one level up, echoing the Indian *file* (diversity.md §6).
- *Prediction 3:* at least one documented tension where a state access regime (open government) and a community stewardship regime constrain the same records in opposite directions.

**What would disconfirm it.** Every coded clause maps cleanly onto access or appraisal already (dimension is redundant); or the frameworks turn out to govern licences/persons rather than records (wrong object, not a new dimension).

**Main feasibility risk.** Source authority and permission: community-governed frameworks are often living documents with versioning and custodial norms about how they are used; coding them extractively without community engagement risks the decolonial critique the idea is meant to answer. Mitigation: desk-only version, or community review.

**Repo connections.** EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY "kinds of document law" (4 kinds + 3 regime dimensions) · diversity.md §10b (trace/access/appraisal), §10d (four custody models), §12d (access as document law), §6 (unit larger than the document) · STANDARDS.md Layer 2 (records-management standards) · analysis/theme_matrix.csv "custody / archive = 16".

**Ethics status.** **Ethics review required** for any version involving consultation with or identification of living communities/ knowledge holders; a purely desk-based coding of published framework texts is not applicable, but must be confirmed before fieldwork of any kind.

## P08-3 — The colonial genealogy test: does documentary form follow the metropole?

**Statement (idea).** Code each system's colonial administrative heritage as a categorical variable and test, by permutation, whether metropole–successor slot-profile similarity exceeds successor–same-region similarity — turning the repo's narrative "imperial/colonial continuity" theme into a measurable claim.

**Mechanism / rationale.** The atlas asserts continuity narratively (Senegal holds the AOF fonds beside the post-1960 state's — diversity.md §11d) and has a deliberate non-colonial contrast (Ethiopia — §10e), yet `analysis/findings.md` §4 computes Jaccard similarity with no genealogy variable at all, and the theme appears in `theme_matrix.csv` only as a keyword count (6 files). One added column on the existing 28-system slot matrix turns "the empire's paperwork becomes the nation's memory" into a falsifiable structural claim: if documentary form follows colonial administration, successor profiles should sit nearer their metropole than nearer their geographical neighbours.

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* colonial-heritage classification of the 28 systems is mostly unambiguous (Francophone/British/Portuguese/Belgian/Italian/none), contested for a few — coding notes required.
- *Assumption:* n ≈ 25 slot-coded systems gives an informative permutation test despite the sparsity artefact already flagged (`JP = KE = HN = 1.0`) — sparse systems must be excluded or down-weighted by a declared rule.
- *Assumption:* metropole profiles are present in the corpus (FR tradition is; GB, PT, BE are not — may need 2–4 thin new case files).

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* mean Jaccard(metropole, successor) > mean Jaccard(successor, same-region non-successor), permutation p < .05.
- *Prediction 2:* the affinity is stronger for Francophone successors, whose tradition fixes *formulas and naming* (cheap to carry) than for successors of layout-fixing traditions (infrastructure-bound).
- *Prediction 3:* Ethiopia (no metropole) shows no such affinity and behaves like a random pair.

**What would disconfirm it.** Affinity indistinguishable from permutation null; or a model comparison showing geography or legal family explains profile similarity better than colonial heritage does.

**Main feasibility risk.** Small n and uneven coding depth (several African systems are thin/secondary-grounded); adding metropole systems means new case files, which lengthens the timeline beyond "reuses frozen data".

**Repo connections.** analysis/findings.md §4 (similarity structure) and §7 · requisites.csv (225 rows) · analysis/slot_similarity.csv + analysis/recompute.py (permutation code can be added as a derived, gated check) · diversity.md §11d, §10e, §9 (open item: Francophone-African vs Commonwealth analytic work) · cases/sn.md, cases/et.md, cases/ng.md, cases/ke.md, cases/za.md · analysis/theme_matrix.csv "imperial / colonial continuity = 6".

**Ethics status.** Not applicable — no human participants.

## P08-4 — Authentication relocation: object-bound, event-bound, system-bound

**Statement (idea).** Type the atlas's most variable slot by *where the binding force resides* — object-bound (signature, registered seal, tughra), event/performance-bound (witness attestation, belt recitation), system-bound (cryptographic signature, automatic log) — and test the compensation prediction that when authentication leaves the page, trace (S14) or authority-mark (S4) thickens in its place.

**Mechanism / rationale.** diversity.md §3 already establishes that S12 varies most because it materialises a society's theory of authority, and §12e shows wampum authority living in belt *and* oral recitation rather than in an artifact; qualitative_analysis.md §4 shows S12 migrating again (digital signatures only after 2015). Recoding S12 by carrier rather than by form yields a testable structural implication the atlas has not checked: a slot cannot simply disappear, so its load should be redistributed to neighbouring slots — system-bound authentication should coincide with automatic trace (Estonia), object-bound with page marks, performance-bound with no page at all.

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* S12 carrier type is recoverable from the existing case-file prose (it is narrated but not tabulated — a small derived coding table would have to be built and added to the gate).
- *Assumption:* co-occurrence patterns across ≈25 systems are interpretable despite sparsity (same caveat as `analysis/findings.md` §7).
- *Assumption:* the three carriers are exhaustive enough for a first pass; hybrid cases (tughra fusing S3+S4+S12) are coded as a separate flag, not forced.

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* system-bound S12 cases show S14 present at a higher rate than object-bound cases (compensation).
- *Prediction 2:* performance-bound authentication appears only in non-documentary or non-state records (khipu, wampum, Islamic court witness attestation).
- *Prediction 3:* fused multi-slot authentication marks (tughra-type) are rare and concentrated in pre-modern sovereign cases.

**What would disconfirm it.** No association between carrier type and S14/S4 across systems; or performance-bound authentication found routinely in written state documents (then "carrier" reduces to "form", adding nothing to §3).

**Main feasibility risk.** The carrier variable does not exist in the frozen data — it must be hand-coded from case prose by one researcher, so the result inherits the single-coder caveat the repo already declares for theme coding (no kappa).

**Repo connections.** diversity.md §3 (authentication most variable), §4 (scalars), §12e (authority without writing) · analysis/findings.md §1 (S12 = 17/25) · analysis/qualitative_analysis.md §4 (authentication as the persistent/migrating slot) · morphology.md §5 finding 1 · cases/ee.md, cases/jp.md, cases/islamic_chancery.md, cases/wampum.md, cases/pe.md.

**Ethics status.** Not applicable — no human participants.

## P08-5 — Epistemic coverage audit: is the atlas's breadth a property of the open web?

**Statement (idea).** Build a coverage-gap index against an external universe (states that maintain a documentation/records standard) and test whether the atlas's inclusion decisions and grounding tiers track *source retrievability* — how far a state publishes its standards on an open web — rather than a principled coverage frame.

**Mechanism / rationale.** The README's honest gaps concede heterogeneous grounding (strong primary anchors vs encyclopedia-thin cases) and two structurally blocked sources (RU provisional, UAE index page JS-only); diversity.md §9 lists whole regions still missing (Central Asia, more of Africa). Inclusion was therefore partly decided by what could be fetched, and the open web's geography is not neutral: states whose administrations publish guides online are fetchable, others are not. If retrievability predicts both inclusion and grounding tier, part of the "six continents" breadth is an artefact of availability — a result that would *strengthen* the honesty of the scoped claim; if retrievability predicts nothing, the coverage defence is empirically earned.

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* an external universe list (states with a national documentation/records standard, or UN member states as a fallback) can be assembled from public listings in a later round — **no search performed this round**.
- *Assumption:* retrievability is measurable post hoc (open-web fetch success, JS-only/blocked status, language of publication), with the RU/AE cases as already-documented exemplars.
- *Assumption:* "grounding tier" can be coded from `analysis/evidence_base.csv` type + retrieved flags and the per-case tier notes without re-reading every case.

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* included systems skew toward states whose standards/guides are openly published; odds of inclusion rise with the retrievability proxy.
- *Prediction 2:* encyclopedia-tier grounding concentrates among states with weak open publication of administrative guidance.
- *Prediction 3:* the largest coverage gaps sit in Central Asia, the Pacific and small states — regions where the current 28 have near-zero representation.

**What would disconfirm it.** Inclusion and tier show no association with retrievability once region and legal family are controlled — i.e. coverage was design-driven, not availability-driven.

**Main feasibility risk.** Circularity: retrievability observed *after* selection is known can bias the proxy; needs a pre-declared proxy definition and a tolerance for a null result that is still publishable (a coverage index with an honest "no availability bias" verdict).

**Repo connections.** README "Honest gaps" + status line (57/60 retrieved, 4 provisional) · analysis/evidence_base.csv (id, type, retrieved) and analysis/findings.md §5 · cases/ru.md, cases/ae.md · diversity.md §9 (still to add) · EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY "What remains" · analysis/recompute.py (a coverage index can be made a gated, derivable output).

## P08-6 — The third declension: does the deliberative note break the two-genre fork?

**Statement (idea).** Test whether the atlas's two declensions conceal a third genre class — the deliberative/argumentative *note* (Indian secretariat note, French/Bénin note de service, U.O. note) — with its own exclusion profile, by functionally coding note-type documents across the corpus and checking them against the S6⊥S9 fork.

**Mechanism / rationale.** The model admits exactly two declensions, defined by which two slots swap (morphology.md §3), and `analysis/findings.md` §2 reports the fork as perfect complementary distribution on 25 systems. But two case files describe a genre that fits neither pole cleanly: the Indian *note* exists "to argue toward a decision" with no European counterpart (`cases/in.md`), and Bénin's *note* drops both formulas and, by rule, S6 and S12 (`cases/bj.md`) — while `cases/francophone_tradition.md` lists note administrative / note de service as core internal genres. If such documents cluster as a stable third profile (body + reference filled; destination, preamble *and* authentication empty), genre is not binary and the famous negation is a special case of a fuller paradigm; if they scatter onto the two existing poles, a failed challenge strengthens the fork.

**Assumptions.**
- *Assumption:* a functional definition (a document whose purpose is to argue toward a decision, issued internally) can identify the genre across systems — necessary because `analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §3 shows five culturally organised naming logics with no shared vocabulary, so a lexical search for "note" would fail.
- *Assumption:* the `genre` column of `requisites.csv` can be extended with a new value without disturbing `analysis/recompute.py`'s checked figures (a schema-gate change, declared as a deviation).
- *Assumption:* ≥5 of the 28 systems contain such a genre — otherwise the hypothesis dies on coverage, not on structure.

**Predicted observations.**
- *Prediction 1:* note-like deliberative documents are identifiable in ≥5 systems across at least three traditions (Indian, Francophone, and one more — e.g. a Soviet/CIS протокол-adjacent or Chinese呈).
- *Prediction 2:* they systematically lack S12 (advice is not binding) and lack S9 (they argue, they do not justify a norm).
- *Prediction 3:* they fill S7/S8 (reference, subject) more often than the normative pole does — a "working document" profile oriented to the file rather than to the public.

**What would disconfirm it.** Notes distribute onto the epistolary and normative profiles with no residual cluster (permutation/cluster check on slot profiles); or the genre cannot be located outside the two already-described cases (coverage failure).

**Main feasibility risk.** Cross-linguistic genre identification by a single coder, on a small n, with several candidate systems thinly sourced; the result could be an artefact of one researcher's functional definition.

**Repo connections.** morphology.md §3 (two declensions) and §5 finding 2 · analysis/findings.md §2 (S6 ⊥ S9, both = 0) · analysis/qualitative_analysis.md §1 (implication rules), §3 (naming logics) · diversity.md §6 (the *file* and the Indian note), §12c (genre by subtraction) · cases/in.md, cases/bj.md, cases/francophone_tradition.md · requisites.csv `genre` column.

**Ethics status.** Not applicable — no human participants.
