# Case: Estonia — the document after paperwork

The far endpoint: a state that has moved the document's slots into
infrastructure.

- **Fact:** as of December 2024, Estonia became the first country to digitise
  100% of its government services [S-ESTONIA-STORY, C42].
- **Mechanism:** digital signing through the national eID stack — ID-card,
  Mobile-ID and Smart-ID — with documents carried in the **DigiDoc** container
  (an ASiC-E file), and notarisation moving to the **e-notary** [S-ESTONIA-ID,
  C43]. Citizens sign state documents from anywhere ("Estonia is with me,
  everywhere" — Kersti Kaljulaid) [S-ESTONIA-STORY].

## What happens to the slots

| slot | paper regime | Estonian regime |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| S2 identifier | written on the page | assigned by the system |
| S6 destination | addressed on paper | resolved by registry lookup |
| S12 authentication | hand signature / seal | cryptographic signature (eID) |
| S14 trace | hand-stamped marks | automatic audit log |
| S13 annex | attached sheets | linked objects in the container |
| whole form | a printed template | a service interface (many forms disappear) |

The **once-only principle** — the state may not ask a citizen for information
it already holds — dissolves whole document genres that exist only to move the
same data between offices. Estonia is the case where the *bureaucratic* slots
(S2, S14) do not disappear but become invisible infrastructure.

## The point for the atlas

Estonia answers the question the atlas started from from the opposite end:
instead of asking what a document *must contain*, it asks whether the
document *must exist*. It shows the 14 slots are not fixed by nature but by
the medium: change the medium (paper → signed container → service) and S2 and
S14 migrate from the page into the state's information system. The morphology
survives; its carriers do not.
