# Case: European Union — Interinstitutional Style Guide

- **Instrument:** the Interinstitutional Style Guide (Publications Office of
  the EU), first published 1997, binding across all EU institutions, bodies,
  offices and agencies [S-EUISG].
- **What it fixes:** the structure of a legal act, among much else.

## Structure of a legal act (section 2)

- **Title of the act** — S1 identity.
- **Preamble** (2.2):
  - **Citations** (2.2.1) — the legal basis, i.e. the treaty articles under
    which the act is adopted. Carries S9 (setting) *and* S7 (reference).
  - **Recitals** (2.2.2) — the "whereas" clauses that justify the act.
- **Articles / enacting terms** (2.3) — the binding operative text, S11.
- **Annexes** — subordinate matter, S13.
- Plus signature / authentic-language clause (S12).

## The interesting point

The EU act is the purest *normative declension* in the sample: it has a
developed setting slot (citations + recitals), a developed dispositif
(articles), a developed annex slot, and **no destination slot at all** — an EU
regulation is addressed to no named addressee; it binds whoever falls within
its scope. The preamble here is not ornament: the citations are the act's
legal competence and the recitals are its justification, both of which are
essential to validity.
