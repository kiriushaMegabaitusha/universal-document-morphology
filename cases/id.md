# Case: Indonesia — Tata Naskah Dinas (ANRI)

- **Instrument:** Peraturan ANRI (National Archives of Indonesia) No. 5 of
  2025 on *Tata Naskah Dinas* — the regulation of the types, format and
  handling of official documents, replacing the earlier general guideline
  (ANRI No. 5/2021, *Pedoman Umum Tata Naskah Dinas*) [S-ID-ANRI, C52].
- **Naskah Dinas** is defined as *written information used as a tool* for the
  execution of official duties — the definition makes explicit that the
  document is instrumental, a working object, not a mere record.

## A three-branch taxonomy

ANRI divides naskah dinas into [S-ID-ANRI, C53]:

1. **Naskah Dinas arahan** — directive documents (orders, instructions).
2. **Naskah Dinas korespondensi** — correspondence, split into **internal**
   and **external** (e.g. Surat Dinas).
3. **Naskah Dinas khusus** — special documents.

## Elements

The prescribed elements include: **kop naskah dinas** (letterhead, in
capitals), **nomor**, **tanggal**, **perihal** (subject), **tembusan** (copy /
CC list, placed at the bottom-left margin), the **penanda tangan**
(signatory), and — already in the 2025 standard — the **tanda tangan
elektronik** (electronic signature) [S-ID-ANRI, C53].

## The point for the atlas

Two things stand out. First, Indonesia organises documents by **function
first** (directive / correspondence / special) rather than by form — a
functional taxonomy, closer to a records-classification scheme than to a
letter-layout standard. Second, the electronic signature is already baked into
the national document standard: like Brazil's Sidof, Indonesia shows a
bureaucracy legislating for the digital document directly, not migrating to it
later.

The same slot set appears (origin, number, date, subject, addressee, trace),
but the frame is archival-classificatory rather than chancery-formal.
