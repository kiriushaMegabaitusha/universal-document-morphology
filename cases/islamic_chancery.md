# Case: the Islamicate chancery (Mamluk and Ottoman)

Two connected bodies of practice, twelve centuries apart, sharing a
non-Western document discipline.

## Al-Qalqashandi's Ṣubḥ al-Aʿshā (Mamluk Egypt, 1412)

- Al-Qalqashandi (1355–1418) was **Kātib al-Darj**, "Scribe of the Scroll",
  a clerk of the Mamluk chancery in Cairo. His Ṣubḥ al-Aʿshā fī Ṣināʿat
  al-Inshāʾ — "Daybreak for the Night-Blind regarding the **Composition of
  Chancery Documents**" — is a fourteen-volume administrative encyclopedia
  completed in 1412 [S-QALQASHANDI, C23].
- It has been called "one of the final expressions of the genre of Arabic
  administrative literature". It is the Islamicate counterpart of European
  diplomatics: a systematic manual for producing state documents, written by
  a working chancery clerk.
- **The chancery manual is also a cryptology text.** Al-Qalqashandi's work
  gives the first published description of a polyalphabetic cipher — letter
  substitution tables and letter-frequency analysis — quoting Ibn al-Durayhim
  [S-QALQASHANDI, C24]. The office that composes documents is, in the same
  book, the office that keeps them secret: composition (S10) and
  confidentiality (S2) are one institution.

## The Ottoman firman

- A **firman** (Persian *farmān*) was an imperial decree carrying the force of
  unappealable law; in the Ottoman Empire, firmans were collected into
  *kanun* [S-FIRMAN, C25].
- **Authentication = the tughra**: a calligraphic monogram, seal *and*
  signature of the sultan in one object, designed at the start of his reign
  [S-TUGHRA, C26]. This is the atlas's clearest case of S3 (origin), S4
  (authority-mark) and S12 (authentication) **fused into a single glyph**.
  Comparable to the Egyptian cartouche and the British royal cypher.
- **Rank is encoded by proportion, not by a slot.** "The more blank space at
  the top of the document, the more important the firman was"
  [S-FIRMAN, C27]. A scalar prestige gradient — a feature a binary
  present/absent slot model cannot capture.
- Body written in the specialised *Divani* calligraphic script on elongated
  parchment scrolls [S-FIRMAN, C28].

## Also relevant

Islamic court procedure gave written documents a distinct evidential status,
and the *shurūṭ* (notarial-formulary) literature codified contract and
document form; authentication there rests on witnesses and named scribes
rather than on a sovereign seal.

## The point for the atlas

The Islamicate chancery shows the same functional slots (origin, setting,
body, authentication, date) disciplining a completely different script,
religion and legal order. It also adds two things the European set cannot:
a **fused** authentication/origin glyph (tughra), and **rank as a scalar
layout variable** (blank space). See `../diversity.md` §3–4.
