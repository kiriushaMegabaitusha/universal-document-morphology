# Case: Kenya — Public Archives and Documentation Service Act

- **Instrument:** the Public Archives and Documentation Service Act (Cap. 19,
  originally 1965), establishing the **Kenya National Archives and
  Documentation Service (KNADS)** and providing for the preservation of public
  archives and records [S-KE-ACT, C58].

## What it foregrounds

From its own section list:

- establishment of KNADS;
- the Director's powers over public records;
- a **national documentation and information retrieval service**;
- **public access to public archives**;
- destruction and export of records (controls);
- **legal validity of public archives**;
- **certified copies admissible as evidence**.

## The point for the atlas

Kenya combines two dimensions the other African cases split between:

1. **Access** — a statutory *right of public access* to archives. Where
   Thailand standardises the trace layer and Nigeria the custody layer, Kenya
   legislates the **access layer**: who may see the record. Access is a
   dimension the atlas's 14 slots do not carry at all (it is a property of the
   regime, not of the document), which is why `diversity.md` now treats
   "access" as a scalar alongside rank — see the `morphology.md` scalar
   dimension discussion.
2. **Evidential value** — certified copies admissible as evidence, i.e. the
   record's S12 (authentication) extended into the courtroom.

Kenya also names a "documentation and information retrieval service" — the
archive framed as a *retrieval system*, an early phrasing of what Estonia would
later build natively.

## Slot profile

Layer-2 regime. Emphasis: S12 (legal validity / admissibility), S14
(custody), plus the **access** dimension (not a document slot).
