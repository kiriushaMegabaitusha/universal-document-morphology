# Case: Nigeria — the National Archives Act

A statutory records regime of unusual breadth.

- **Instrument:** the National Archives Act, establishing the National
  Archives of Nigeria and defining responsibilities for the preservation and
  management of public records [S-NG-ACT, C57].
- **What it covers** (from its own table of provisions):
  - establishment and general functions of the National Archives;
  - **responsibilities for records management**, and the designation of a
    **departmental records management officer**;
  - power of the Director to examine public records, and to **requisition
    records in peril**;
  - **criteria for the selection of public records for permanent
    preservation** (appraisal);
  - preservation of constitutional and legal documents;
  - **legal validity of public records**;
  - establishment of State Archives by State Governments;
  - preservation of records of **private bodies** and of **company records**
    (business houses may establish their own archives).

## Why it matters

The Act reaches **beyond the state**: it legislates for private archives and
for company records, and it gives the Director power to seize records at risk.
That breadth — the state asserting a custodial interest in non-state
documentation — is broader than most European records acts and broader than
South Africa's governmental-body remit.

In the atlas's terms, Nigeria is a **Layer-2** regime (records management, not
page layout) with a strong S14 (trace/custody) and S12 (legal validity)
emphasis, plus an explicit **appraisal** function — the criteria by which a
document is chosen to survive. Appraisal is the slot-model's blind spot: it
governs not what a document *contains* but whether it *persists*.

## Slot profile

Layer-2 regime (no page slots). Governs persistence and custody: heavy S14
(trace, appraisal, requisition) and S12 (legal validity), applied across
public, private and company records.
