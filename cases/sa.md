# Case: Saudi Arabia — المراسلات الكتابية (administrative correspondence)

- **Instrument:** the *دليل المراسلات الكتابية* (guide to written
  correspondence) used in Saudi government practice, e.g. the KSU/Ministry-of-
  Finance guide [S-SA-KSU, C61]. It is a chancery manual in the classical
  sense: it prescribes not only the slots but the *formula* for each rank.

## The elements

The guide prescribes the standard set: **ترويسة** (letterhead), **الرقم**
(number), **التاريخ** (date), **المرسل** (sender), **المرسل إليه**
(addressee), **الموضوع** (subject), **التحية** (salutation), **الصيغة /
المتن** (body), **المرفقات** (attachments), **التوقيع** (signature),
**الخاتم** (seal) and **نسخة** (copy) [S-SA-KSU, C61]. The slot set is
unremarkable and maps cleanly onto the atlas's model — what is remarkable is
how the slots are *filled*.

## Four rank-driven mechanisms

1. **Addresses are ordered by protocol rank.** When a letter goes to several
   people, they are listed in a fixed hierarchy: the Custodian of the Two Holy
   Mosques, then the Crown Prince, then Their Highnesses (الأصحاب السمو), then
   Their Excellencies — ministers first — then other Excellencies, then their
   Honours, and within each class by seniority of appointment [S-SA-KSU, C62].

2. **The salutation is rank-dependent.** After the addressee's name the guide
   prescribes a blessing that varies with rank: *حفظه الله ورعاه* for the
   Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques, *حفظه الله* for the Crown Prince and
   Highnesses, *سلّمه الله* for others — followed for all by the Islamic
   greeting *السلام عليكم ورحمة الله وبركاته*; omitted entirely for internal
   letters [S-SA-KSU, C63].

3. **The calendar is Hijri-first.** The official calendar is the Hijri
   (Umm al-Qura); the date runs day/month/year, and the Gregorian date follows
   with *الموافق* (corresponding to) — e.g. 1440/4/12 هـ الموافق 2018/12/20 م
   [S-SA-KSU, C64].

4. **The opening formula is conditioned on the rank gap.** Citations of a
   prior instrument differ by type: a royal decree, high order or Council of
   Ministers decision is followed by *القاضي بـ* ("which stipulates"), a mere
   letter by *المتضمن* ("containing"), and the cited number must be exact — an
   error in the reference number may invalidate the letter's content
   [S-SA-KSU, C65]. The transition into the body uses *مقامكم الكريم / سموكم /
   معاليكم* according to the addressee's rank, and *أفيدكم* when the addressee
   is junior to the sender [S-SA-KSU, C66].

## The point for the atlas

Saudi practice shows the atlas's **rank dimension** doing real grammatical
work. Rank is not a field on the page (cf. the Ottoman firman's blank space);
here it enters the *language itself*: which blessing follows the name, which
possessive (*مقامكم / سموكم / معاليكم*) addresses the reader, which verb opens
the sentence. It is the most thorough case in the atlas of hierarchy encoded
in register rather than in layout.

It also gives the Arab chancery's **instrument typing**: the guide distinguishes
the مرسوم ملكي (royal decree), أمر سامي (high order), قرار (decision) and تعميم
(circular) — an S1 (identity) taxonomy that determines which citation verb the
letter must use.
