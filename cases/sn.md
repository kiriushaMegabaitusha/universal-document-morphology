# Case: Senegal — the Francophone administrative model and the AOF archive

- **Instruments:** Senegal's public administration runs on the shared
  **Francophone administrative model** (see `francophone_tradition.md`); the
  distinctive Senegalese evidence is the *circulaire* and the national archive.

## The circulaire

Scholarship on Senegalese administrative law defines the circulaire
precisely [S-SN-CIRC, C70]:

> Par circulaires ou instructions de service, on désigne des communications
> par lesquelles un supérieur hiérarchique, normalement le ministre, fait
> connaître à ses subordonnés ses intentions sur un point relatif [au
> service].

Two features matter for the atlas:

1. **Directionality is built into the type.** A circulaire runs *down* the
   hierarchy (superior → subordinates); it is not a letter between equals. The
   document type encodes the organisational relation, exactly as the Saudi
   instrument types encode rank.
2. **The name encodes the rank of the issuer.** A circulaire "prend la
   qualification de l'autorité qui l'a édictée": one speaks of *circulaires
   présidentielle, primatorale, ministérielle, préfectorale*. The document's
   identity (S1) is derived from who signed it.

## The archive

The **Direction des Archives du Sénégal** holds, as named fonds
[S-SN-ARCH, C71]:

- **Sénégal Colonial** and **Afrique Occidentale Française (AOF)** — the
  documentary record of the French imperial administration in West Africa;
- **Sénégal Contemporain** — the fonds of governments since 1960;
- the **Journal Officiel** and the législative/regulatory texts.

## The point for the atlas

Senegal is where the *empire's paperwork becomes the nation's memory*: the
same archive holds the colonial administration's AOF fonds and the
post-independence state's fonds, under one national institution. The document
regime is continuous across the 1960 rupture even though the sovereignty is
not.

The circulaire also completes a pattern seen in Saudi Arabia and Thailand: the
**document type is a rank statement**. In Senegal the type name carries the
issuing authority; in Saudi Arabia the type (royal decree / high order /
decision) likewise fixes the authority; in both, S1 (identity) is not neutral
metadata but a claim about who has the power to say this.
