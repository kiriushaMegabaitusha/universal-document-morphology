# Case: the Soviet bloc — the unified documentation regime

The missing system-wide regime: not one country's format but a **supranational
programme to standardise official documents across a whole bloc**, and the
direct ancestor of both ГОСТ Р 7.0.97 (RU) and ДСТУ 4163 (UA).

## Timeline

| year | instrument | what it did |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1920 | Central Institute of Labour (CIT), Gastev | scientific organisation of labour (НОТ) — rationalisation prehistory [S-SU-GASTEV, C89] |
| 1960s | АСУ (automated management systems) drive | unification of documents for machine processing |
| 1973 | **ЄДСД** (approved by ГКНТ USSR No. 435) | the state's clerical system [S-SU-EDSD, C83] |
| 1972/1990 | **ГОСТ 6.38-72 → 6.38-90** (УСОРД) | the unified *requisite* standard [S-SU-GOST638, C86] |
| 1988 | **ГСДОУ** | its successor doctrine |
| — | **ОКУД** | the all-Union classifier of management documents [S-SU-OKUD, C87] |

## The architecture: three layers fixed at once

The Soviet regime standardised three things simultaneously — which is why it is
the most ambitious document regime in the atlas:

1. **The requisite set** (ГОСТ 6.38-90): emblem, date, index, addressee,
   approval and agreement endorsements, heading, text, access restriction —
   numbered requisites, in force 1991-01-01, with the rule of **no more than
   four addressees** [S-SU-GOST638, C86]. ДСТУ 4163:2020 still carries this
   four-addressee rule, three decades later.
2. **The document-type taxonomy** (ОКУД): a three-level hierarchical
   classification of management-document forms with an **8-digit code** —
   class (2) + subclass (2) + ordinal (3) + **check digit** (1) — created to
   register forms, reduce their number and remove non-unified ones
   [S-SU-OKUD, C87]. This is a document-type schema with a checksum: the state
   as a database designer.
3. **The workflow** (ЄДСД): unified rules for composing and formatting
   documents, rational document-flow principles, a unified registration-
   reference apparatus, nomenclatures and file formation, preparation of files
   for storage, and — written into the doctrine in 1973 — "mechanisation and
   automation of clerical work" [S-SU-EDSD, C84].

The **УСД** programme framed these as "unified documentation systems" built on
uniform rules, with the organisational-administrative system (УСОРД) as one of
approximately sixteen such systems, registered in the state classifier
[S-SU-USD, C88].

## The cybernetic reading

The Soviet regime is the clearest case in the atlas of paperwork conceived as
**systems engineering**: a named "unified system", a classified type-table with
check digits, and automation built into the founding doctrine fifty years
before digital government. It is the ЕГСД's 1960s АСУ context — documents
unified *so that machines could process them* — that makes the 2020s "rules as
code" movement look like a return rather than a novelty.

## Legacy across the bloc (polycentric, not one country's)

- **Retained and evolved:** Russia (ГОСТ Р 7.0.97-2016, see `ru.md`), Ukraine
  (ДСТУ 4163:2020, see `ua.md`), and the CIS/Central Asian states on the ГОСТ
  line. In Ukraine the doctrine lives on in the *Типова інструкція з
  діловодства* (Постанова КМУ No. 1242 of 2011, superseded by Постанова
  No. 55 of 2018) [S-SU-EDSD, C85].
- **Escaped:** the **Baltic states** — Estonia's document regime is EU/eID
  (see `ee.md`), a deliberate break. Eastern Europe moved to the EU
  Interinstitutional Style Guide (see `eu.md`).
- **Bloc dimension:** COMECON pursued "uniform technical, legal and statistical
  standards" among members [S-SU-COMECON, C90] — the attempt to make the
  standardisation supranational.

## The document-type classification

The bloc's second layer — the type taxonomy — organises documents **by
function** into three groups: *organisational* (статут, положення,
інструкція, договір), *dispositive* (наказ, вказівка, ухвала,
розпорядження, рішення) and *reference-informational* (акт, протокол,
довідка, лист) [S-SU-ORD, C92]. The Ukrainian national classifier НК 010:2021
(in force 2021-04-19, replacing ДК 010-98) is the living successor to the
all-Union ОКУД and enumerates the individual types — акт, наказ, протокол,
довідка, статут, положення, договір, пояснювальна записка and others
[S-SU-NK010, C91]. So the Soviet/CIS tradition answers "what kind of document
is this?" by **documentary function**, a fifth naming logic alongside the
authority/function/ritual/format logics of the other traditions.

## The point for the atlas

The Soviet bloc is the counter-case to the atlas's "convergent skeleton,
divergent skin" thesis: here the *skeleton itself* was legislated for a bloc,
in numbered requisites and classified type-codes. It also explains a family
resemblance the atlas had observed but not sourced — why ДСТУ 4163 and ГОСТ Р
7.0.97 look so alike (shared ЄДСД/ГОСТ 6.38 ancestor, including the
four-addressee rule). And it shows a bloc regime **fragmenting**: Russia,
Ukraine, the Baltics and Central Asia inherited the same ancestor and diverged
by political choice, not by accident.

## Slot profile

Governs all three layers: requisites (S2 ● index/access, S4 ● emblem, S5 ●
date, S6 ○ addressee, S8 ● heading, S10 ● text, S12 ● endorsements), the
classifier (S2 ● / meta-structure ●), and the workflow (S14 ● registration
apparatus, nomenclatures, storage; infrastructure ● mechanisation/automation).

## Source-quality note

The ЄДСД and ГОСТ 6.38 material is grounded on Ukrainian-hosted sources
(Ukrainian Wikipedia; a Ukrainian standard mirror) because Russian hosts are
unavailable from this environment; the ОКУД and УСД details come from secondary
Ukrainian educational/dictionary sources and are flagged as such. The 1920
prehistory and the COMECON dimension rest on English-language sources.
