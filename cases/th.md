# Case: Thailand — งานสารบรรณ (the Sarabun regulations)

- **Instrument:** the Regulation of the Office of the Prime Minister on
  *งานสารบรรณ* (clerical/registry work), B.E. 2526 (1983) and its amendments —
  the Thai state's rulebook for official correspondence [S-TH-SARABUN, C54].
  Thai has a dedicated word for this documentary craft, *sarabun* (สารบรรณ).

## The external letter (หนังสือภายนอก) and its elements

The Regulation defines หนังสือภายนอก (external letter — formal, on
**ครุฑ (Garuda)**-stamped paper) with a fixed element list per แบบที่ ๑
(Form 1) [S-TH-SARABUN, C54]:

| element | meaning | slot |
|---------|---------|------|
| ที่ | number / owner's code | S2 |
| ส่วนราชการเจ้าของหนังสือ | issuing agency | S3 |
| (date) | date | S5 |
| เรื่อง | subject | S8 |
| (salutation) | to whom | S6 |
| อ้างถึง (ถ้ามี) | reference, if any | S7 |
| สิ่งที่ส่งมาด้วย (ถ้ามี) | enclosures, if any | S13 |
| ข้อความ | body | S10 |
| ลงชื่อ | signature | S12 |
| (position, agency, โทร) | position, agency, phone | S12 / S14 |

Plus a companion **internal letter** (หนังสือภายใน, a less formal Form 2) and
a whole family of numbered forms (แบบที่ 3 … 26) for orders, announcements,
minutes, etc. [S-TH-SARABUN, C55].

## The Garuda and the registry

- The state emblem, the **ครุฑ (Garuda)**, is printed on official paper with
  prescribed dimensions (1.5 cm / 3 cm; the round seal 4.5 / 3.5 cm
  diameters) [S-TH-SARABUN, C55]. An S4 authority-mark fixed to the
  millimetre.
- The Regulation also prescribes the **registers** — ทะเบียนหนังสือรับ /
  ทะเบียนหนังสือส่ง (incoming and outgoing letter registers), ใบรับหนังสือ
  (receipt forms), บัตรตรวจค้น (search cards) — i.e. it standardises the
  **trace layer** as rigorously as the document itself [S-TH-SARABUN, C55].

## The point for the atlas

Thailand is the clearest case of a **bureaucracy standardising its own
trace/audit layer** alongside the page format: the incoming/outgoing register
is a first-class, legally prescribed artifact. Where DIN 5008 ignores S14 and
Ukraine fills it with hand-stamps, Thailand gives it its own printed forms.
And the Garuda is an authority-mark embedded in the *paper itself* (paper
stamped with the emblem), not a slot on the page — the emblem is a property of
the medium.
