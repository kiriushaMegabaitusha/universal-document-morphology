# Diversity — the world beyond the European standard-and-imperial axis

`morphology.md` established the universal stem from six systems that are,
frankly, related: UA/RU share a Soviet ancestor; DE, UN and EU all inherit
Western legal culture; CN is the one genuine outsider. That is not enough to
claim universality. This file tests the model against traditions that did not
inherit from Rome, from the European chancery, or from the Westphalian state —
and against the two endpoints of the medium, the knotted cord and the signed
digital container.

*Post-B3 note (2026-10-28).* "Universal" in this file is read as **system-level
recurrence**. The registered B3 lineage audit (`lineage/RESULTS.md`) finds no stem
slot present in every documented lineage and no detectable lineage clustering, so
the stem claim is lineage-conditional; the diversity findings below are unchanged
as census facts, but they do not establish lineage-independent universality.

Sources: `cases/islamic_chancery.md`, `cases/in.md`, `cases/br.md`,
`cases/jp.md`, `cases/ee.md`, `cases/pe.md`, `cases/za.md`.

## 3. Finding: authentication is the most variable slot

Five cultures, five realisations of the same slot (S12):

- **West** — hand signature, historically over a wax seal.
- **East Asia** — the *registered seal* (Japan's hanko): impersonal, officiary,
  a public registry of your seal; harder to forge than a signature.
- **Ottoman** — the *tughra*: one glyph that is simultaneously the sovereign's
  name (S3), the authority-mark (S4) and the signature (S12) — three slots
  fused into one.
- **Islamic courts** — attested by witnesses and named scribes, not by a
  sovereign mark.
- **Estonia** — a cryptographic signature in an eID.

Which slot varies *most* across the world is not layout, subject or date —
those are stable. It is **authentication**, the slot that certifies the
document's binding force. That makes sense: authentication is where a
society's theory of authority is materialised, and theories of authority vary
more than facts about dates.

## 4. Finding: rank can be a scalar, not a slot

The Ottoman firman encoded its own importance as **proportion**: "the more
blank space at the top of the document, the more important the firman was".
The atlas's binary slot model (present/absent) cannot represent this. Some
document properties are **gradients, not slots** — importance, urgency
(China's 紧急程度), secrecy (a graded classification). A fuller model would
carry, alongside the 14 slots, a set of scalar dimensions modulating the whole
document.

## 5. Finding: the model's boundary — the record system without documents

The Inka **khipu** is a decimal positional record system, used for census,
calendar and military administration — with no page, no linear reading order,
no addressee, no signature. Its "morphology" is an *encoding* morphology (cord
hierarchy, knot type, colour), closer to a database schema than to a charter.

This is the honest limit of the universality claim: the 14-slot grammar holds
for the **written official document** across cultures and centuries — not for
record-keeping as such. A khipu is a record system *without* documents, and
forcing the slots onto it is itself the finding.

## 6. Finding: the record unit can be one level up — the file

India's CSMOP shows a recordkeeping unit larger than the document: the
**file**, with its own four-part anatomy (correspondence portion, notes
portion, appendix to correspondence, appendix to notes) and its own identifier
grammar (a faceted functional file number). The atlas's model is
document-centric; in the Indian secretariat the accountable object is the
file, and the *note* (a document whose sole purpose is to argue toward a
decision) is a genre with no counterpart in the European sample.

## 7. Finding: the medium moves the slots into infrastructure

Estonia (100% digital public services, Dec 2024) is the far endpoint. S2
(identifier) is assigned by the system; S12 is a cryptographic signature; S14
(trace) becomes an automatic log; and the *once-only principle* dissolves
whole genres that exist only to shuttle the same data between offices. The
morphology survives; its carriers do not. Brazil's Sidof shows the same shift
one generation earlier: the format becomes the schema of the national
e-document system.

## 8. What this changes in `morphology.md`

- The universality claim is **strengthened diachronically** (Islamicate
  chancery, 1412, same slots) but **bounded** (khipu, n/a).
- A 15th "slot" is added as a *dimension*, not a box: scalar rank / urgency /
  secrecy (finding §4).
- The recordkeeping unit is recognised as possibly larger than the document
  (finding §6).
- The centre of gravity of cross-cultural variation moves to **S12**
  (authentication), not S6/S9 as the European-only comparison suggested.

## 10. The Southeast Asian and African tranche (added second pass)

### 10a. Finding: the state's self-description can be a slot

Vietnam's *Quốc hiệu và Tiêu ngữ* puts the country's constitutional name
**and its motto** at the head of every document (Nghị định 30, component 1).
No European standard has an equivalent: it is an authority-mark (S4) that is
**discursive**, not graphic — the document recites the state's
self-description. Thailand's Garuda goes the other way: an authority-mark
that is a property of the *paper itself* (ครุฑ-stamped stock, dimensioned to
the millimetre), not a slot laid on the page. S4 spans from a printed motto to
the physical substrate.

### 10b. Finding: three dimensions the policy regimes add — trace, access, appraisal

The African statutory regimes, read together, expose three things the 14 slots
do not carry:

- **Thailand** standardises the *trace* layer as first-class artifacts — the
  incoming/outgoing registers, receipt forms, search cards are printed forms.
  S14 is here as substantial as the document.
- **Kenya** legislates an *access* layer (statutory public access to archives).
  Access is the inverse of trace: it governs who may read the record. The atlas
  now carries it as a scalar dimension alongside rank (see §4).
- **Nigeria** legislates an *appraisal* layer — the criteria by which a
  document is chosen to survive. Appraisal governs not what a document
  contains but whether it persists; no page-format standard touches it.

So a document's life needs three policy dimensions beyond its morphology:
rank (how important), access (who may read), persistence (whether it survives).

### 10c. Finding: the Latin-script objection is dead

West African **Ajami** documents — correspondence and business records in
Hausa, Fulfulde, Wolof, Songhay and others, written in a modified Arabic
script — show administrative documents whose script is neither Latin nor
European, following Islamicate documentary conventions. This was the last
serious alternative explanation for the atlas's "universality"; it is now
covered. (The khipu remains the one boundary case, §5.)

### 10d. Finding: four custody models, and their fragility

The African and Andean cases give four distinct answers to "who keeps the
record?":

| model | case | risk |
|-------|------|------|
| monastic / ecclesiastical | Ethiopia | dispersed; dependent on the church |
| family / private household | Timbuktu / Ajami | unprotected — 4,203 manuscripts burned in the Mali War |
| statutory state archive | Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa | strong on paper, weak in practice |
| automatic system log | Estonia | durable but out of human view |

### 10e. Finding: Ethiopia — a non-colonial chancery

Ethiopia is the strongest single counter-example to "universal = European": a
sovereign, never-colonised state with its own script (Ge'ez/Fidäl), its own
imperial correspondence and its own custody tradition (monastic), producing
documents the slot model recognises. It is the case that most needs a dedicated
study rather than a one-page entry.

## 11. The Arab-standards and Francophone tranche (added third pass)

Cases: `cases/sa.md` (Saudi Arabia), `cases/ae.md` (UAE, provisional),
`cases/sn.md` (Senegal), `cases/francophone_tradition.md` (the shared model,
grounded on Luxembourg and French guides plus the French royal chancery).

### 11a. Finding: rank can be encoded in the language itself

The atlas already had rank as a **scalar dimension** (the Ottoman firman's blank
space). Saudi correspondence pushes it further: rank is encoded
*grammatically*. Which blessing follows the addressee's name (حفظه الله ورعاه /
حفظه الله / سلّمه الله), which possessive addresses them (مقامكم / سموكم /
معاليكم), and which verb opens the sentence are all selected by the rank gap
between sender and addressee. Rank moves from layout into register.

### 11b. Finding: the formula, not the slot, can be the standardised object

The Francophone tradition standardises **language**: banks of permitted
*formules d'appel* and *formules de politesse*, indexed by letter type and by
addressee rank, with a hard matching rule between salutation and close, and
canonical phrases (*«J'ai l'honneur de…»*). Where the Anglophone/Germanic
standards fix layout and the Sinosphere fixes an element grid, the Francophone
tradition fixes the phrases. It descends directly from the medieval chancery
formulary (royal chancery typology: charte / lettre patente / mandement).

### 11c. Finding: the document type is a statement of rank

In Saudi Arabia the instrument type (مرسوم ملكي / أمر سامي / قرار) determines
which citation verb the letter must use (القاضي بـ vs المتضمن); in Senegal the
circulaire "prend la qualification de l'autorité qui l'a édictée"
(présidentielle / primatorale / ministérielle / préfectorale); Thailand's forms
are ranked too. Across all three, S1 (identity) is not neutral metadata — it is
a claim about who has the power to say this. The atlas's identity slot is
therefore *institutional*, not descriptive.

### 11d. Finding: the empire's paperwork becomes the nation's memory

Senegal's national archive holds the **Afrique Occidentale Française** colonial
fonds alongside the post-1960 state's fonds, in one institution. The document
regime is continuous across the 1960 rupture even though sovereignty is not.
The archive is where a former colony possesses — and must steward — the
administrative record of its own colonisation.

- **Nordic transparency** (Sweden 1766) and **wampum** — **now done** (§12).

## 12. Closing the open items (added fourth pass)

Cases resolved or added: `cases/ae.md` (UAE — resolved from a real primary
source), `cases/bj.md` (Bénin), `cases/se.md` (Sweden), `cases/wampum.md`
(Haudenosaunee wampum).

### 12a. Finding: a standard can fix the reference apparatus

The UAE's TDRA guide does not standardise correspondence slots; it standardises
**how to name and cite things** — the state's name and symbols, the official
titles of the rulers, the names of official bodies, and citations, dates,
numbers and time. That is a third kind of document standard, alongside layout
(Anglophone/Germanic) and formulas (Francophone): a **reference discipline**.
It tells you what you are allowed to call things.

### 12b. Finding: regional template diffusion is real

Chasing a "second Gulf standard", a Qatar-hosted legal library was found to
publish a guide **byte-identical to the Saudi one** (same MD5). The Saudi
دليل المراسلات الكتابية therefore circulates regionally as the Gulf's de
facto template. Lesson recorded as a method: check the fingerprint before
treating a re-hosted document as an independent standard — this is how a
"there is no separate Qatari standard to find" result gets discovered honestly
instead of invented.

### 12c. Finding: genre-by-formula, proven by subtraction

Bénin makes the atlas's genre axis explicit by *subtraction*: the internal
**note** carries **neither formule d'appel nor formule de politesse** — the two
slots that define the external **lettre**. Within one tradition, a genre is
defined by which formulas it drops. This is the cleanest single demonstration
that the atlas's "epistolary vs normative" fork (§3) is really a formalism
about which slots are filled.

### 12d. Finding: the access regime is a fourth kind of document law

Sweden's *offentlighetsprincipen* (1766, enshrined in a fundamental law) fixes
neither layout, nor formulas, nor naming: it fixes **who may read the record**,
three centuries before the Anglophone FOI acts. Kenya legislates the same
dimension statutorily; Sweden embeds it constitutionally. Together they settle
that "access" is not a document slot but a property of the surrounding legal
order.

### 12e. Finding: authority does not require writing

The Haudenosaunee wampum gives a second boundary case (with the khipu) and a
sharper point: for the Two Row Wampum, the **belt and the oral recitation
carry the authority**, and the later *written* versions are the disputed ones.
The document-centric assumption that writing is where authority lives is
inverted. Two independent non-documentary record systems now bound the atlas's
claim: the universal grammar holds for the *written* official document, not for
record-keeping as such.

### 12f. Where the atlas now stands

27 systems, six continents, c. 1412–2024. The catalogue of "kinds of document
law" now runs: layout (Anglophone/Germanic/Sinosphere), formulas
(Francophone), naming (UAE), plus the three regime dimensions — trace (Thailand,
Ukraine), access (Sweden, Kenya), persistence/appraisal (Nigeria) — and the two
non-documentary boundaries (khipu, wampum).

## 13. The Soviet bloc (added fifth pass)

Case: `cases/soviet_bloc.md` — the one *supranational* regime in the atlas, and
the missing ancestor of both ГОСТ Р 7.0.97 and ДСТУ 4163.

### 13a. Finding: the bloc legislated all three layers at once

Where other systems fix one thing (layout, or formulas, or naming), the Soviet
regime fixed three simultaneously: the **requisite set** (ГОСТ 6.38-90), the
**document-type taxonomy** (ОКУД), and the **workflow** (ЄДСД). It is the most
ambitious document regime in the corpus — a state-wide unification of
formatting, typing and process in a single doctrine.

### 13b. Finding: the classifier is a document-type schema with a checksum

ОКУД assigns every management-document form an **8-digit code** — class (2) +
subclass (2) + ordinal (3) + **check digit** (1) — explicitly to register
forms, reduce their number and eliminate non-unified ones. This is the state
acting as a database designer: a document-type schema with integrity checking,
five decades before machine-readable legislation.

### 13c. Finding: automation was written into the doctrine in 1973

ЄДСД's own components include "mechanisation and automation of clerical work",
and the surrounding 1960s **АСУ** drive unified documents *so that machines
could process them*. The 2020s "rules as code" movement is thus a return to a
1960s–70s position, not a novelty — a correction to the atlas's implicit
"digital = 2018+" dating.

### 13d. Finding: the bloc fragmented by choice, not accident

Russia (ГОСТ Р 7.0.97-2016), Ukraine (ДСТУ 4163:2020) and the CIS/Central Asian
states inherited the ЄДСД/ГОСТ line; the **Baltic states deliberately escaped**
to the EU/eID model; Eastern Europe moved to the EU style guide; COMECON had
pushed "uniform technical, legal and statistical standards" supranationally
[S-SU-COMECON]. One ancestor, many successors — divergence by political choice.

### 13e. Finding: the four-addressee rule is a measurable fossil

ГОСТ 6.38-90 limited a document to **four addressees**; ДСТУ 4163:2020 still
carries the identical rule three decades later. The atlas had observed the
ДСТУ/ГОСТ resemblance without sourcing it; the Soviet case supplies the
ancestor and a concrete inherited rule.

### 13f. Finding: the bloc classified documents by function

Its type taxonomy groups documents by **documentary function** —
organisational / dispositive / reference-informational — and the Ukrainian
classifier НК 010:2021 (successor to the all-Union ОКУД) enumerates the types
(акт, наказ, протокол, довідка, статут, положення…). This closes the atlas's
onomastic gap and adds a fifth naming logic to the type-lexicon (see
`analysis/qualitative_analysis.md` §3).
